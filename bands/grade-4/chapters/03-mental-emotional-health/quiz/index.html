    Quiz: Mental and Emotional Health¶

    Test your understanding of self-regulation, emotions, bias, and responding safely to teasing and bullying with these review questions.

    1. What is self-regulation?¶

    1. The ability to notice a strong feeling and choose how to respond to it
    2. Hiding your feelings so nobody notices them
    3. A rule that says you can never feel upset
    4. Letting a strong feeling control what you do next
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Self-regulation is not about hiding feelings or never feeling upset — it means noticing what you feel, naming it, and picking a helpful next step instead of letting the feeling take over.

    Concept Tested: Self-Regulation

    2. Why does mental health change over time?¶

    1. Because it is fixed forever once a person is born
    2. Because only physical health can change, not mental health
    3. Because life events, growth, stress, and relationships can shift emotional well-being up or down, just like physical health
    4. Because mental health only changes if a person does something wrong
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Just like physical health, mental health moves up and down over time due to life events, stress, and relationships. A harder stretch does not mean something is permanently wrong with a person.

    Concept Tested: Mental Health Changes Over Time

    3. What is the purpose of cultural self-regulation practices like mindfulness meditation, prayer, or storytelling?¶

    1. They are only for adults, not students
    2. They are traditional methods that help people calm strong emotions and find balance
    3. They replace the need for trusted adults entirely
    4. They only work if a person's whole family uses the same one
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Cultural self-regulation practices are traditional methods passed down through a culture or community that help calm strong emotions. No single tradition is more "correct" than another — they are different paths to the same goal of steadiness.

    Concept Tested: Cultural Self-Regulation Practices

    4. How can feeling connected to your culture and community support your mental health?¶

    1. It can build a sense of belonging and give you more people to turn to for support
    2. It guarantees a person will never feel sad
    3. It only matters for adults, not students
    4. It replaces the need for any other kind of support
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. People who feel connected to their culture and community often have a stronger sense of identity, more people to turn to for support, and traditional coping practices refined across generations, all of which support mental health.

    Concept Tested: Mental Health Benefits Of Culture

    5. Why is it important to remember that emotions influence behavior?¶

    1. Because behavior is always automatic and cannot be changed
    2. Because only negative emotions lead to behavior
    3. Because it means feelings and actions are exactly the same thing
    4. Because noticing an emotion early gives you a chance to choose your next action instead of reacting without thinking
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Emotions often lead to actions, but the emotion and the behavior are two different steps. Noticing a feeling early, like frustration, gives you the chance to choose a helpful behavior, like asking for help, instead of reacting without thinking.

    Concept Tested: Emotions Influence Behavior

    6. Malik feels embarrassed after tripping in the hallway, and a few classmates laugh. Which response shows healthy management of that emotion?¶

    1. Snapping angrily at the nearest classmate
    2. Pretending the embarrassment never happened and never thinking about it again
    3. Taking a slow breath, reminding himself that everyone trips sometimes, and moving on
    4. Avoiding the hallway completely for the rest of the school year
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Healthy strategies for managing emotions include naming the feeling, using a calming strategy like slow breathing, and giving yourself a moment before deciding how to act. This helps the feeling pass without leading to an unhelpful behavior.

    Concept Tested: Managing Emotions

    7. What is bias?¶

    1. A fixed, negative opinion a person refuses to change even with new evidence
    2. An unfair leaning toward or against a person or group, often based on limited information
    3. A type of bullying that happens online
    4. A healthy way to manage strong emotions
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Bias is an unfair leaning toward or against a person or group, often without even realizing it, usually based on limited or incomplete information. Everyone can develop biases, and noticing them is the first step toward being fair.

    Concept Tested: Bias

    8. A teacher assumes a new student won't be good at math just because the student speaks a different language at home, and keeps thinking this even after the student does very well on several tests. How is this different from a quick, unnoticed bias?¶

    1. It isn't different at all — bias and prejudice always mean the exact same thing
    2. It is less serious because the teacher is an adult
    3. It is only a problem if the student finds out about it
    4. It has become prejudice, because the negative opinion is being held onto even when evidence proves it wrong
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Bias can be a quick, sometimes unnoticed assumption, but prejudice is a stronger, fixed belief that persists even when evidence shows it is untrue. Holding onto the unfair opinion despite proof it's wrong is what makes this prejudice rather than simple bias.

    Concept Tested: Prejudice

    9. Sofia notices that a group of classmates has been leaving her out of games at recess for the past few days. What is the best first step for Sofia to try?¶

    1. Leave the school grounds without telling anyone
    2. Yell at the classmates in front of the whole class
    3. Calmly say how it made her feel, like "I feel left out when I'm not invited. Can I join?"
    4. Assume nothing can be done and stop going to recess
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A calm, clear statement about how the situation made you feel is often a good first step for teasing or exclusion. If it continues, walking away and telling a trusted adult are the next safe steps. No student is responsible for exclusion aimed at them.

    Concept Tested: Responding To Teasing And Exclusion

    10. Which resource is an example of culturally appropriate help for a student's mental health?¶

    1. A community health worker who speaks the family's home language, or a school counselor trained to work with students from many backgrounds
    2. Only ever talking to a stranger online
    3. A rule that everyone must talk to the exact same type of person
    4. Ignoring the problem until it goes away on its own
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Culturally appropriate help means support that respects a person's cultural background, language, and family practices. This can include a school counselor, a family elder, a religious leader, or a community health worker — whoever helps a student feel heard, safe, and respected.

    Concept Tested: Culturally Appropriate Help
