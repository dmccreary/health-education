    Quiz: Substances - Effects and Safer Choices¶

    Test your understanding of substance effects on the brain and body, Indigenous perspectives, coping strategies, and safe medication practices with these review questions.

    1. Why does the chapter emphasize that fentanyl contamination in counterfeit pills is a serious factual concern rather than an exaggeration?¶

    1. CDC data documents that counterfeit pills are frequently contaminated with fentanyl, and this contamination cannot be detected by sight, taste, or smell, making a single use of an unknown pill a real overdose risk
    2. Fentanyl contamination only affects pills purchased from unfamiliar sources, never any others
    3. This risk applied a decade ago but no longer applies to the current drug supply
    4. Fentanyl can always be detected by its distinct taste before use
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter states this is documented public health information from the CDC, not a scare tactic: counterfeit pills and some batches of other illegal drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl, and there is no way to visually, tastefully, or otherwise detect this contamination before use, making a single use a real overdose risk.

    2. Why do public health organizations deliberately use the term "commercial tobacco" rather than just "tobacco" in prevention messaging?¶

    1. Because commercial tobacco and traditional tobacco use are identical practices
    2. Because the term has no specific purpose and is used interchangeably with all tobacco use
    3. Because it targets manufactured, addiction-engineered products specifically, without casting judgment on sacred ceremonial or spiritual tobacco use
    4. Because traditional tobacco use has been proven equally harmful to commercial products
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter explains that public health organizations, including many Indigenous-led health programs, use "commercial tobacco" specifically so that prevention messaging about addiction and disease targets manufactured, profit-driven products without stigmatizing a sacred cultural and spiritual practice that differs fundamentally in purpose, preparation, and frequency.

    3. Why does nicotine dependence form faster and more intensely in an adolescent brain than in an adult brain exposed to the same amount?¶

    1. Adolescent brains are less sensitive to nicotine than adult brains
    2. Nicotine only affects the brain of people over the age of twenty-five
    3. The brain circuits involved in attention, learning, mood, and impulse control are still being built during adolescence, making them more vulnerable to nicotine's effects on the reward system
    4. Adolescents metabolize nicotine more slowly than adults, reducing its effects
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter explains that adolescent nicotine exposure changes the wiring of brain circuits involved in attention, learning, mood, and impulse control, circuits that are still being built during the teenage years, which is why dependence can form faster and more intensely than in a fully matured adult brain.

    4. In the holistic Indigenous wellness framework described in the chapter, how is substance use disorder generally understood?¶

    1. As entirely unrelated to any dimension of a person's life
    2. As purely an isolated physical problem with no connection to other dimensions
    3. As solely a moral failing requiring individual willpower to overcome
    4. As a sign that balance has been disrupted across one or more dimensions — physical, mental, emotional, or spiritual — rather than an isolated physical or moral problem
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter describes a holistic framework, sometimes represented as a medicine wheel with four connected quadrants, that treats substance use disorder as a sign that balance has been disrupted across one or more of these dimensions, rather than as an isolated physical or moral problem.

    Concept Tested: Indigenous Views Of Health And Substance Use

    5. A student feels overwhelmed with stress before finals and wants an evidence-based, substance-free way to cope. Which strategy does the chapter identify as producing a documented, short-term stress reduction?¶

    1. Physical activity, such as a short walk or workout, which reduces stress hormones and increases mood-supporting chemicals
    2. Avoiding all contact with friends and family until the stress passes
    3. Ignoring the stress entirely and hoping it resolves on its own
    4. Staying awake later to fit in more study time regardless of sleep needs
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter identifies physical activity as a strategy that measurably reduces stress hormones and increases the body's own mood-supporting chemicals, with even a short walk or workout producing documented short-term stress reduction, as one of several evidence-based, substance-free coping options.

    Concept Tested: Coping Strategies Without Substances

    6. A person is prescribed an opioid pain reliever after a procedure and is deciding whether it's safe to have a drink of alcohol while taking it. Based on the chapter, what is the correct guidance?¶

    1. It is always safe as long as only one drink is consumed
    2. Alcohol and opioids only interact if taken at the exact same moment
    3. Never combine alcohol with a medication unless a prescriber or pharmacist has specifically confirmed it is safe, since alcohol combined with opioids can intensify sedation and slow breathing to dangerous levels
    4. Combining the two is only risky for people under the age of eighteen
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter explains that alcohol interacts with many medications, including opioid pain relievers, in ways that can intensify sedation and slow breathing to dangerous levels, and states the safe default is never combining alcohol with a medication unless a prescriber or pharmacist has specifically confirmed it is safe.

    Concept Tested: Medication Interaction Risks

    7. A patient is using a traditional plant-based remedy alongside medication prescribed by their doctor. According to the chapter, what is the correct safety practice?¶

    1. Assume the remedy is automatically safe since it is natural and requires no disclosure
    2. Tell every healthcare provider about the remedy, the same way they would disclose an over-the-counter supplement, so the provider can check for interaction risk
    3. Stop using the traditional remedy entirely rather than mentioning it to a provider
    4. Only disclose the remedy if the provider directly asks about traditional medicine
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter states that a person should tell every healthcare provider about any traditional or plant-based remedy being used, the same way they would disclose a supplement, because some plant compounds can interact with prescription medications in ways a provider needs to know to prescribe safely.

    Concept Tested: Traditional Medicine Use Considerations

    8. A student's ongoing nicotine dependence disrupts their sleep, which leads to declining grades, which increases their stress, which in turn increases their substance use further. What does this sequence illustrate?¶

    1. A single, isolated effect that does not connect to any other area of life
    2. A cycle in which effects on relationships, school, and work compound each other, and which is easier to interrupt earlier than after it has been reinforced for months or years
    3. Evidence that substance use has no measurable effect on school performance
    4. A pattern that only affects adults, not adolescents
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter describes exactly this compounding pattern: disrupted sleep and concentration from substance use can lead to declining grades, increased stress, and further substance use, forming a cycle that is easier to interrupt early than after it has been reinforced over time.

    Concept Tested: Substance Use Effects On Relationships School And Work

    9. According to the chapter, which specific cognitive process is most directly impaired when a person's prefrontal cortex is affected by substance use, making it harder to accurately judge the likelihood and severity of a negative outcome?¶

    1. Risk assessment
    2. Long-term memory storage of childhood events
    3. Basic motor coordination unrelated to any decision
    4. Language comprehension in a first language
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter specifically names impaired risk assessment as one of the documented effects of substances that affect the prefrontal cortex, reducing a person's ability to accurately judge the likelihood and severity of a negative outcome, which is one reason high-risk behaviors increase under the influence.

    10. A friend claims that warnings about fentanyl contamination are "probably overblown" and just meant to scare teenagers away from trying anything. Evaluating this claim using the chapter's evidence, what is the strongest response?¶

    1. The friend is right, since fentanyl contamination has never been documented by any public health agency
    2. The claim cannot be evaluated because no data exists on this topic
    3. The warning only applies to adults, so it doesn't matter for teenagers
    4. This is a documented public health fact supported by CDC data, not an exaggeration, and stating it plainly is different from sensationalizing it
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter directly addresses this exact claim, stating that CDC data documents fentanyl contamination in counterfeit pills as a real, current public health fact, not an exaggeration, and that presenting this evidence plainly is different from using fear-based scare tactics. The goal is accurate information, and the evidence supports the fact as stated.
