    Quiz: Emotional Well-Being and Mental Health¶

    Test your understanding of stress management, warning signs of distress, suicide and self-harm risk, and relationship skills with these review questions.

    1. According to this chapter, how can a person reach the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline?¶

    1. By calling or texting 988, anywhere in the United States, 24 hours a day
    2. Only by visiting an emergency room in person
    3. Only during school hours on weekdays
    4. Only after a parent gives permission
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline can be reached by calling or texting 988, anywhere in the United States, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, connecting the caller or texter with a trained crisis counselor for free and confidentially.

    Concept Tested: Seeking Help For Suicide Risk

    2. Which of these is listed in the chapter as a verbal warning sign of suicide risk?¶

    1. Getting a slightly lower grade on one test
    2. Feeling nervous before a big presentation
    3. Talking about feeling like a burden to others, or saying goodbye in a way that feels final
    4. Wanting some extra time alone after a long week
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter lists verbal signs such as talking about wanting to die, feeling hopeless, feeling like a burden to others, or saying goodbye in an unusual, final-feeling way, as signs that call for an immediate, caring, serious response.

    Concept Tested: Suicide And Self-Harm Warning Signs

    3. Why does the chapter say a restorative practice like a short walk or slow breathing only "counts" as restorative under certain conditions?¶

    1. It only works if done for at least two hours at a time
    2. It has to be done regularly as a habit, not just once during a crisis, to reliably reset the body before stress builds up
    3. It only counts if a doctor specifically prescribes it
    4. It works the same whether it is done once or done regularly, so timing doesn't matter
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter states a practice only counts as restorative if done regularly, not just once during a crisis. Building it into a normal week gives the body a reliable way to reset before stress accumulates.

    Concept Tested: Restorative Stress-Management Practices

    4. How does the chapter describe the relationship between Indigenous and traditional healing approaches and conventional mental health support like counselors?¶

    1. They are complementary — many people draw on both rather than choosing one instead of the other
    2. Traditional healing approaches should replace conventional counseling whenever possible
    3. Conventional counseling is always more effective than traditional approaches
    4. The two approaches conflict and should never be used together
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter explains that traditional healing practices are valid, time-tested complements to conventional mental health support, not alternatives to it — a community's traditional practices and a school counselor's support are not in competition.

    Concept Tested: Indigenous And Traditional Healing Approaches

    5. A friend has been withdrawing from activities, sleeping poorly, and seeming persistently sad for three weeks. What makes this a warning sign of emotional distress rather than an ordinary bad stretch?¶

    1. Any change in mood at all automatically counts as a mental health emergency
    2. It only counts as a warning sign if the friend says so directly
    3. Three weeks is too short a time period to notice any real pattern
    4. It reflects a lasting pattern of change across multiple areas of the friend's life, not a single hard day
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter emphasizes that emotional distress shows up as a change from a person's normal pattern that lasts and affects multiple areas of life, not as a single bad day. Persistent sadness and behavioral change over weeks fits that pattern.

    Concept Tested: Warning Signs Of Emotional Distress

    6. A student feels a surge of anger during a group project and wants to avoid saying something they'll regret. Which strategy from the chapter fits this moment best?¶

    1. Immediately telling the group exactly how frustrated they are, without pausing
    2. Pausing for a few seconds or breaths before reacting
    3. Ignoring the feeling completely and pretending it isn't there
    4. Waiting for the emotion to pass on its own without using any strategy
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter names pausing before reacting as a specific emotion management strategy: taking a few seconds or breaths between feeling an emotion and acting on it prevents a reaction a person might later regret.

    Concept Tested: Emotion Management Strategies

    7. A friend tells a student they've been feeling hopeless and asks them to promise not to tell anyone. Applying the chapter's guidance, what should the student do?¶

    1. Promise secrecy first so the friend feels safe, then decide later whether to tell someone
    2. Wait a few days to see if the friend brings it up again before doing anything
    3. Tell the friend to keep it to themselves since it might just be a passing phase
    4. Listen without judgment, refuse to promise secrecy, and get a trusted adult involved immediately
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter is explicit that this is the one kind of promise that should never be made. The correct response is to listen without judgment, tell the friend directly that you care too much to keep it secret, and involve a trusted adult right away.

    Concept Tested: Seeking Help For Suicide Risk

    8. During a disagreement, a student says, "I feel frustrated when plans change last-minute," instead of "You always ruin plans." What communication technique is being applied?¶

    1. An "I" statement, which describes feelings and needs instead of accusing the other person
    2. Active listening, which is about reflecting back what someone else said
    3. Nonviolent conflict resolution's adult-involvement step
    4. A restorative stress-management practice
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter names "I" statements as describing one's own feelings and needs rather than accusing the other person, which reduces defensiveness during a disagreement.

    Concept Tested: Communication For Conflict Resolution

    9. Two classmates have a disagreement over a group project. In Scenario 1, it's a one-time disagreement about roles. In Scenario 2, one classmate has repeatedly been excluding and mocking the other in front of the class for weeks. According to the chapter, how should these two situations be handled differently?¶

    1. Both situations should always be resolved directly between the two classmates without adult involvement
    2. Scenario 1 fits peer-level nonviolent conflict resolution, while Scenario 2 involves a pattern that may need a trusted adult, since it resembles bullying or a power imbalance
    3. Both situations require immediate adult intervention with no attempt at peer resolution
    4. Scenario 2 is less serious because it doesn't involve a single dramatic moment
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter's nonviolent conflict resolution steps note that conflicts involving safety, bullying, or a serious power imbalance need adult involvement rather than being handled between peers alone, while an ordinary one-time disagreement fits the peer-level resolution path.

    Concept Tested: Nonviolent Conflict Resolution

    10. Comparing two friends: Friend A had one rough day after a bad grade but bounced back by the next day. Friend B has stopped talking to friends, is sleeping poorly, and has seemed hopeless for a month. What does analyzing these two cases show, based on the chapter?¶

    1. Both friends are experiencing the same level of concern and need the identical response
    2. Friend A's situation is more concerning because a bad grade is a clear, specific cause
    3. Friend A's experience is an ordinary stressor, while Friend B shows a pattern of warning signs that deserves a caring, serious response
    4. Neither situation needs any response since teenagers naturally have mood swings
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A single hard day, like Friend A's, does not indicate emotional distress on its own. Friend B's month-long pattern of withdrawal, poor sleep, and hopelessness across multiple areas of life fits the chapter's description of warning signs that call for a caring, serious response and connection to support.
