    Quiz: Substances and the Developing Brain¶

    Test your understanding of substance use disorder, medication misuse, substance effects on the developing brain, and protective factors with these review questions.

    1. According to health organizations cited in this chapter, how is substance use disorder classified?¶

    1. As a temporary bad habit that resolves on its own without treatment
    2. As a chronic medical brain disease, not a sign of weak willpower or a character flaw
    3. As a personal choice unrelated to brain chemistry
    4. As a condition that only affects adults, never adolescents
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Health organizations including the American Medical Association and NIDA classify substance use disorder as a brain disease involving real, physical changes in the brain's reward and decision-making circuits, similar in structure to how diabetes reflects a physical change in blood sugar management.

    Concept Tested: Substance Use Disorder

    2. How does the chapter describe relapse in the context of recovery from substance use disorder?¶

    1. Clear proof that treatment does not work and should be stopped
    2. A rare event that almost never happens during recovery
    3. A moral failure that reflects a person's lack of effort
    4. A common, expected part of the recovery process for many chronic conditions, not a sign of failure
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter compares relapse to a flare-up in asthma or a rise in blood pressure readings — a signal to adjust treatment, not evidence that treatment failed or that the person is at fault.

    Concept Tested: Relapse

    3. Why does the chapter say the adolescent brain is more vulnerable to substance effects than an adult brain?¶

    1. The reward system matures early while the prefrontal cortex, responsible for judgment and impulse control, does not fully develop until the mid-20s
    2. Adolescent brains process chemicals identically to adult brains, so there is no real difference
    3. The prefrontal cortex finishes developing before puberty begins
    4. The reward system in adolescents is less active than in adults
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter describes a mismatch between an early-maturing reward system and a prefrontal cortex that isn't fully developed until a person's mid-twenties, which makes introducing substances during adolescence more disruptive and more likely to lead to dependence.

    Concept Tested: Alcohol Effects On Developing Body And Brain

    4. How does the chapter distinguish commercial tobacco from the traditional, ceremonial use of tobacco practiced by many Indigenous nations?¶

    1. There is no meaningful difference between the two, and both carry identical health risks
    2. Traditional ceremonial tobacco use is more addictive than commercial tobacco products
    3. Commercial tobacco is manufactured and marketed for profit and engineered to maximize dependence, while traditional use is a sacred, respectful cultural and spiritual practice
    4. Commercial tobacco refers only to tobacco grown outside the United States
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter draws this distinction carefully: commercial tobacco products are manufactured and engineered for profit and dependence, while traditional and ceremonial tobacco use is a distinct spiritual and cultural practice, not the subject of the chapter's health concerns.

    Concept Tested: Commercial Tobacco Effects On Developing Body And Brain

    5. Why does the chapter identify illegal drugs as carrying a distinct, additional risk compared to alcohol, nicotine, or cannabis?¶

    1. Illegal drugs are actually less potent than legal substances
    2. They are unregulated, so a person cannot know for certain what a substance actually contains or how strong it is, including possible fentanyl contamination
    3. Illegal drugs do not affect the brain's reward system at all
    4. Illegal drugs carry no legal consequences, only health risks
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter highlights that illegal drugs are unregulated, meaning there is no reliable oversight of their contents or strength, including a documented rise in poisoning deaths linked to fentanyl being mixed into other drugs without the user's knowledge.

    Concept Tested: Illegal Drug Effects On Developing Body And Brain

    6. A student takes a friend's prescription anti-anxiety medication before a test because they heard it helps with nerves. How does the chapter classify this?¶

    1. Misusing prescription medication, which is dangerous because the medication was prescribed for a specific person's body chemistry and condition
    2. A safe practice, since anti-anxiety medication is harmless to share between friends
    3. Misusing an over-the-counter medication, since anti-anxiety medication doesn't require a prescription
    4. Not a form of misuse, since it was only used once
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Taking someone else's prescription medication is prescription medication misuse. The chapter explains this is genuinely dangerous, not just against the rules, because a medication safe for one person can be harmful for another due to differences in body chemistry and health history.

    Concept Tested: Misusing Prescription Medications

    7. A student takes two different cold medicines at the same time, not realizing both contain the same pain-relieving ingredient. Based on the chapter, what risk does this create?¶

    1. No risk at all, since both products are available without a prescription
    2. A risk only if the medicines are taken more than a week apart
    3. An accidental overdose, since combining products with the same active ingredient can exceed a safe dose without the person realizing it
    4. A risk only for adults, not for adolescents
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter specifically warns that combining multiple OTC products sharing the same active ingredient can result in an accidental overdose, even though each product is generally safe when used exactly as directed on its own.

    Concept Tested: Misusing Over-The-Counter Medications

    8. A student learns that a family friend in recovery from substance use disorder recently relapsed after two years of sobriety. Applying the chapter's guidance, what is the most helpful response?¶

    1. Conclude that the person's treatment must not have worked and give up on encouraging future treatment
    2. Avoid mentioning it at all, since relapse should never be discussed
    3. Treat the relapse as a moral failure that reflects poorly on the person's character
    4. Encourage the person to reconnect with treatment or support, the same way you would encourage someone managing any other chronic condition
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter states that relapse is a signal to return to treatment or adjust support, not a reason to give up. Encouraging reconnection with treatment is the most helpful response, just as it would be for any other chronic health condition.

    Concept Tested: Relapse

    9. A student who feels connected to their cultural community, has clear family expectations about substance-free choices, and has a mentoring relationship with an elder is compared to a student without these connections. What does the chapter's research on protective factors suggest?¶

    1. Protective factors have no real, documented effect on substance use risk
    2. The first student has multiple documented protective factors that research links to a lower likelihood of developing substance use problems
    3. Only family expectations matter; cultural connectedness and elder relationships have no research support
    4. Having multiple protective factors guarantees a person will never use substances
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter identifies cultural connectedness, family and community expectations, and connection to elders as separate, evidence-supported protective factors that research links to lower rates of substance use, though they reduce risk rather than guarantee an outcome.

    Concept Tested: Cultural Teachings That Protect Youth

    10. Comparing cannabis and illegal drugs like illicitly manufactured fentanyl using the chapter's risk framework, what is the key difference in risk profile?¶

    1. Cannabis and illegal drugs carry identical risk profiles in every category
    2. Cannabis carries no developmental brain risk at all, while illegal drugs carry all the risk
    3. Illegal drugs are safer because they are less commonly used than cannabis
    4. Both carry developmental brain risk, but illegal drugs carry substantially higher unpredictability and contamination risk since they are entirely unregulated
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter's comparison shows both cannabis and illegal drugs carry high developmental brain risk, but illegal drugs carry severe unpredictability and contamination risk, since their contents and strength cannot be verified at all.

    Concept Tested: Illegal Substance Consequences
