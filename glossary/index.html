    Glossary of Terms¶

    Ability-Inclusive Kindness¶

    Treating people with kindness and fairness regardless of any physical, sensory, or learning differences they have, and including them in activities and conversations.

    Example: Inviting a classmate who uses a wheelchair to join a game and adjusting the rules so everyone can play.

    Used in: Grade 5

    Accessibility Of Health Resources¶

    The degree to which health services, information, and supports are easy for people to reach, afford, and use, regardless of location, income, ability, or language.

    Example: A community clinic that offers free interpreters and evening hours is more accessible to working families.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Active Listening¶

    Paying full attention to a speaker, showing interest through eye contact and body language, and responding in ways that show understanding.

    Example: Nodding and repeating back what a friend said before offering your own opinion.

    Used in: Grade 2, Grade 3

    Adapting Health Behaviors¶

    Changing personal habits or routines in response to new information, life changes, or health goals in order to maintain or improve well-being.

    Example: Switching from sugary snacks to fruit after learning about the effects of sugar on energy levels.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Addressing Exclusion And Bullying¶

    Recognizing when someone is being left out or bullied and taking direct action, such as speaking up, including them, or reporting the behavior.

    Example: Telling a teacher after seeing a classmate repeatedly excluded from group activities.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Addressing Stereotyping And Discrimination¶

    Identifying unfair generalizations or unequal treatment based on group identity and responding with actions or words that challenge them.

    Example: Correcting a friend who assumes all members of a group share the same interests.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Adolescent Access To Health Care¶

    The ability of teenagers to obtain medical services, including how factors like cost, transportation, and confidentiality affect whether they seek care.

    Example: A teen clinic that lets students schedule appointments without a parent present makes care easier to access.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Adolescent Healthcare Access Rights¶

    The legal protections that allow teenagers to seek certain health services, sometimes without parental consent, depending on state law.

    Example: In some states, teens can receive confidential treatment for mental health or reproductive care.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    AED Use¶

    The steps for operating an automated external defibrillator, a portable device that checks heart rhythm and can deliver a shock to restore a normal heartbeat.

    Example: Following the AED's voice prompts to place pads on a person's chest during cardiac arrest.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Alcohol Effects On Developing Body And Brain¶

    The ways alcohol can disrupt growth, judgment, memory, and brain development in children and teens, whose bodies are still maturing.

    Example: Alcohol use during adolescence can interfere with the brain regions responsible for decision-making.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Alcohol Misuse Consequences¶

    The physical, social, legal, and academic problems that can result from drinking alcohol in unsafe or underage ways.

    Example: Getting suspended from a sports team after being caught drinking at a school event.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Alcohol Short- And Long-Term Effects¶

    The immediate impacts of alcohol, such as impaired coordination and judgment, along with the lasting health effects of continued use, such as liver damage.

    Example: A single night of heavy drinking can impair reaction time, while years of heavy drinking can damage the liver.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Allergic Reaction¶

    The body's immune response to a substance it mistakenly treats as harmful, which can cause symptoms ranging from mild itching to severe swelling.

    Example: A child's skin breaking out in hives after eating peanuts.

    Used in: Grade 5

    Allergic Reaction Signs¶

    The warning symptoms that indicate someone is having an allergic reaction, such as hives, swelling, trouble breathing, or itching.

    Example: Noticing a classmate's lips swelling after eating a snack and telling an adult right away.

    Used in: Grade 5

    Allergy¶

    A condition in which the body reacts strongly to a normally harmless substance, such as certain foods, pollen, or insect stings.

    Example: Some people sneeze and get watery eyes when they are near cats because of an allergy.

    Used in: Grade 4

    Allergy Prevention And Management¶

    Steps taken to avoid allergy triggers and to respond safely when a reaction occurs, such as reading food labels or carrying medication.

    Example: A student checking snack labels for peanuts and keeping an epinephrine auto-injector nearby.

    Used in: Grade 4

    Alzheimer's Disease¶

    A brain disease that gradually destroys memory and thinking skills, most often affecting older adults.

    Example: A grandparent with Alzheimer's disease may forget the names of close family members over time.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Alzheimer's Disease And Other Dementias¶

    A group of brain conditions, including Alzheimer's disease, that cause a progressive decline in memory, reasoning, and the ability to perform daily tasks.

    Example: Dementia can make it hard for a person to remember how to get home from a familiar place.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Anxiety Symptoms And Causes¶

    The physical and emotional signs of excessive worry or fear, such as a racing heart or restlessness, along with the stressors or situations that can trigger them.

    Example: A student may feel a tight chest and racing thoughts before a big exam due to test anxiety.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Asking A Trusted Adult¶

    Going to a parent, teacher, or other reliable grown-up for help, guidance, or information when needed.

    Example: Telling a teacher when you feel unsafe on the playground.

    Used in: Grade 1

    Asking For Help¶

    Telling another person, especially an adult, that you need support or assistance with a problem.

    Example: Raising your hand to ask a teacher for help when you don't understand an activity.

    Used in: Kindergarten

    Asking For Help With Body Changes¶

    Reaching out to a trusted adult with questions or concerns about the physical changes that happen as a child's body grows.

    Example: Asking a parent why you are getting taller and your voice sounds different.

    Used in: Grade 3

    Asking For Help With Choices¶

    Seeking guidance from a trusted adult when trying to decide what to do in a difficult or confusing situation.

    Example: Asking a parent for advice before deciding whether to join a new after-school activity.

    Used in: Grade 1

    Balanced Meal Planning¶

    Choosing foods from different food groups so a meal provides a healthy mix of nutrients the body needs.

    Example: Planning a dinner with vegetables, whole grains, protein, and fruit instead of only dessert.

    Used in: Grade 4

    Behavioral And Environmental Factors In Disease Risk¶

    The personal habits and surrounding conditions, such as diet, exercise, and pollution, that can raise or lower a person's chance of developing a disease.

    Example: Living near heavy traffic pollution combined with smoking both raise the risk of lung disease.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Belonging¶

    A feeling of being accepted, valued, and included as part of a group or community.

    Example: Feeling like part of the class when teammates cheer for you during a game.

    Used in: Grade 1, Grade 5

    Benefits Of Being Substance-Free¶

    The physical, mental, and social advantages of not using tobacco, alcohol, or other drugs, such as better health and clearer thinking.

    Example: Athletes who avoid substances often recover faster and perform more consistently.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Benefits Of Physical Activity¶

    The positive effects that regular movement and exercise have on the body and mind, such as stronger muscles and better mood.

    Example: Running at recess can make you feel more awake and happy for the rest of the day.

    Used in: Grade 1, Grade 3

    Benefits Of Physical Activity On Mood¶

    The way that exercise and movement can improve how a person feels emotionally, often by reducing stress and increasing happiness.

    Example: Going for a bike ride after a stressful day can help someone feel calmer.

    Used in: Grade 4

    Beverage Choice Evaluation¶

    The process of comparing drinks based on factors like sugar content, nutrients, and hydration to decide which is the healthiest option.

    Example: Comparing the sugar content of soda, juice, and water before choosing what to drink with lunch.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Beverages For Physical Activity¶

    Drinks, such as water, that are appropriate choices before, during, or after exercise to stay properly hydrated.

    Example: Drinking water during a soccer game instead of a sugary sports drink.

    Used in: Grade 3

    Bias¶

    An unfair preference or judgment about a person or group, often based on limited or inaccurate information.

    Example: Assuming a new student isn't good at sports just because of where they are from.

    Used in: Grade 4

    Body Parts¶

    The different sections and structures that make up the human body, such as the arms, legs, and head.

    Example: Pointing to and naming your elbow, knee, and shoulder.

    Used in: Kindergarten

    Body Safety¶

    Rules and practices that help protect a person's body from harm, including knowing which touches are appropriate and telling a trusted adult about unsafe situations.

    Example: Knowing that no one should touch the parts of your body covered by a swimsuit without a good reason.

    Used in: Grade 1

    Bone-Strengthening Activity¶

    Physical activity that puts force on bones through impact or muscle tension, helping bones grow stronger.

    Example: Jumping rope and playing basketball are both bone-strengthening activities.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Boundary¶

    A personal limit that tells others what behavior is acceptable or not acceptable toward you.

    Example: Telling a friend "please don't read my journal" sets a boundary.

    Used in: Grade 2

    Boundary-Setting¶

    The act of clearly communicating your personal limits to others in a relationship or interaction.

    Example: Saying "I need space right now" when a sibling is bothering you.

    Used in: Grade 5

    Boundary-Setting Strategies¶

    Specific approaches, such as using clear language or body language, for communicating personal limits to others effectively.

    Example: Using a firm voice and direct eye contact to say "stop" when someone crosses a line.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Brain-Healthy Habits¶

    Daily behaviors, such as sleeping enough and limiting substance use, that support healthy brain function and development.

    Example: Getting eight hours of sleep each night supports memory and focus.

    Used in: Grades 6-8, Grades 9-12

    Breakfast¶

    The first meal of the day, typically eaten in the morning, that provides energy after a night without food.

    Example: Eating oatmeal and fruit before school.

    Used in: Grade 1

    Bullying¶

    Repeated, intentional aggressive behavior, physical or verbal, that involves a real or perceived imbalance of power between people.

    Example: A student repeatedly calling a classmate names and encouraging others to exclude them.

    Used in: Grade 3

    Bullying Effects On Mental Health¶

    The emotional and psychological harm, such as anxiety or low self-esteem, that can result from being bullied.

    Example: A student who is bullied over time may start to feel anxious about coming to school.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Bystander¶

    A person who witnesses a bullying incident, conflict, or unsafe situation without being directly involved as the target or the one causing harm.

    Example: A student who sees another student being teased at lunch but isn't part of the teasing.

    Used in: Grade 5

    Bystander Action Against Bullying¶

    Steps a witness can take when they see bullying occur, such as speaking up, getting help, or supporting the person being targeted.

    Example: Walking over to sit with a student who is being excluded and telling a teacher afterward.

    Used in: Grade 5

    Caffeinated And Energy Drink Effects¶

    The physical impacts of consuming caffeine and energy drinks, such as increased heart rate, disrupted sleep, and jitteriness.

    Example: Drinking an energy drink before bed can make it harder to fall asleep.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Caffeine In Drinks¶

    A natural stimulant found in some beverages, such as soda and tea, that can affect energy levels and sleep.

    Example: Some sodas contain caffeine, which is why they can make you feel more awake.

    Used in: Grade 2

    Cannabis Effects On Developing Body And Brain¶

    The ways cannabis use can interfere with memory, attention, and brain development in children and teens.

    Example: Cannabis use during the teen years can make it harder to concentrate in school.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Cannabis Short- And Long-Term Effects¶

    The immediate effects of cannabis use, such as impaired coordination, along with lasting effects on memory and lung health from repeated use.

    Example: Smoking cannabis can immediately slow reaction time and, over years, can harm the lungs.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Carbohydrates¶

    A nutrient found in foods like bread, rice, and fruit that the body breaks down into sugar for energy.

    Example: Pasta is a food that is high in carbohydrates.

    Used in: Grade 3, Grade 4

    Caring Relationships¶

    Connections with family, friends, or other trusted people that involve kindness, support, and concern for one another's well-being.

    Example: A grandparent who checks in on you and listens when you are upset shows a caring relationship.

    Used in: Grade 1

    Choosing The Healthier Food¶

    The skill of comparing two food options and selecting the one that provides better nutrition for the body.

    Example: Picking an apple instead of a candy bar for a snack.

    Used in: Grade 5

    Choosing The Healthiest Option¶

    The process of comparing several choices, such as foods, activities, or behaviors, and selecting the one most likely to support good health.

    Example: Choosing a salad with grilled chicken over fried food at a restaurant.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Chronic Condition¶

    A health condition that lasts a long time, often a lifetime, and usually requires ongoing management rather than a quick cure.

    Example: Diabetes is a chronic condition that a person manages every day.

    Used in: Grade 5

    Chronic Disease Prevention Strategies¶

    Actions, such as regular exercise and healthy eating, that lower the risk of developing long-lasting illnesses like heart disease or diabetes.

    Example: Eating more vegetables and walking daily can help prevent type 2 diabetes.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Coercion¶

    Pressuring or forcing someone to do something against their will through threats, manipulation, or intimidation.

    Example: Threatening to end a friendship unless someone shares a secret is a form of coercion.

    Used in: Grade 5, Grades 6-8

    Collaborative Goal-Setting Process¶

    A method of setting personal or group goals that involves input, feedback, and agreement from multiple people, such as a student and a mentor.

    Example: A student and coach working together to set a realistic training goal for the season.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Commercial Tobacco Addiction¶

    A dependence on manufactured tobacco products, driven by nicotine, that makes quitting difficult even when a person wants to stop.

    Example: A person who feels irritable and anxious without a cigarette may be experiencing commercial tobacco addiction.

    Used in: Grades 6-8, Grades 9-12

    Commercial Tobacco Effects On Developing Body And Brain¶

    The ways nicotine and other chemicals in manufactured tobacco products can harm growth, lung development, and brain function in young people.

    Example: Nicotine use in adolescence can affect the parts of the brain that control attention and impulse control.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Commercial Tobacco Short- And Long-Term Effects¶

    The immediate effects of using manufactured tobacco products, such as increased heart rate, along with lasting effects like lung disease from continued use.

    Example: Smoking raises heart rate right away and, over years, can lead to lung cancer.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Common Illness Symptoms¶

    The typical warning signs of frequent illnesses, such as fever, cough, or upset stomach, that signal a person may be sick.

    Example: A runny nose and sore throat are common symptoms of a cold.

    Used in: Grade 5

    Communicating And Interpreting Consent¶

    The skill of both clearly expressing agreement or refusal and correctly understanding another person's expressed agreement or refusal in a relationship.

    Example: Recognizing that silence or hesitation is not the same as a clear "yes."

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Communicating Boundaries And Consent¶

    Clearly expressing personal limits and checking for agreement before engaging in an activity with another person.

    Example: Asking "is it okay if I hug you?" before doing so.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Communicating Feelings¶

    Used in: Grade 1

    Communicating Needs¶

    Clearly telling others what you require, whether physical, emotional, or social, so they can understand and respond appropriately.

    Example: Telling a teacher "I need a quiet space to focus."

    Used in: Grade 1

    Communication¶

    The exchange of information, ideas, or feelings between people through speaking, writing, gestures, or listening.

    Example: Talking with a friend about weekend plans is a form of communication.

    Used in: Grade 1

    Communication For Conflict Resolution¶

    Used in: Grades 6-8, Grades 9-12

    Communication In Decision-Making¶

    Community And Cultural Recovery Supports¶

    Community Food Access Initiatives¶

    Community Laws On Substance Use¶

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Community Nutrition Initiatives¶

    Example: A school district partnering with local farms to provide fresh vegetables in cafeterias.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Community Safety Practice¶

    Used in: Kindergarten

    Community Societal And Environmental Factors In Health Practices¶

    Community Values¶

    Example: A community health center that offers free confidential counseling and testing for teens.

    Comparing Drink Choices¶

    The skill of looking at different beverage options and identifying differences in their ingredients or health effects.

    Example: Comparing the sugar amounts listed on a soda label and a milk carton.

    Used in: Grade 2

    Comparing Water To Other Drinks¶

    Used in: Grade 5

    Comprehensive Health Care¶

    A broad range of medical services, including prevention, treatment, and follow-up care, aimed at addressing a person's overall health.

    Example: A clinic that provides checkups, vaccinations, and counseling under one roof offers comprehensive health care.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Conflict¶

    A disagreement or clash between two or more people who have different needs, opinions, or goals.

    Example: Two friends arguing over whose turn it is to choose a game.

    Used in: Grade 2

    Conflict Management¶

    Example: Taking a deep breath and talking it out instead of yelling during an argument.

    Used in: Grade 4

    Conflict Resolution¶

    Used in: Grade 5

    Conflict-Resolution Skill Evaluation¶

    The process of assessing how well a particular approach to resolving a disagreement worked and what could be improved.

    Example: Reflecting on whether compromising or apologizing led to a better outcome after an argument.

    Used in: Grades 9-12

    Conflict-Resolution Skills¶

    Specific abilities, such as active listening and compromise, that help people resolve disagreements peacefully.

    Example: Using "I" statements instead of blame during a disagreement.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Consent¶

    A clear, voluntary, and informed agreement to participate in an activity or interaction with another person.

    Example: Asking a friend if it's okay to borrow their pencil and waiting for them to say yes.

    Used in: Grade 5, Grades 6-8, Grades 9-12

    Consent In Healthy Relationships¶

    The ongoing practice of asking for and respecting agreement between partners before any shared activity, as a foundation of respectful relationships.

    Example: Checking in with a partner before making plans that affect both people.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Consent Versus Coercion¶

    The difference between a freely given agreement and one that results from pressure, manipulation, or force.

    Used in: Grade 5, Grades 6-8

    Consequences Of Sharing Explicit Content¶

    Example: Forwarding a private photo without permission can lead to school discipline or legal charges.

    Used in: Grades 6-8

    Constructing A Health Position¶

    Used in: Grade 4

    Coping Strategies Without Substances¶

    Healthy ways to manage stress or difficult emotions that do not involve using alcohol, tobacco, or other drugs.

    Example: Going for a run or talking to a friend instead of using substances to deal with stress.

    Coping Strategy¶

    A method or action a person uses to deal with stress, difficult emotions, or challenging situations.
