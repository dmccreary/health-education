```text
Type: timeline
sim-id: relationship-norms-timeline

Bloom Taxonomy Level: Analyze (L4)
Bloom Verb: examine, distinguish

Learning objective: Students examine how legal and social recognition of relationship
structures has changed over roughly a century, and distinguish "long-standing" norms from
"recently changed" ones.

Time period: 1920s-present, United States focus with two international comparison points

Events (each with click-to-expand detail):
- 1920s-1940s: Many states enforce anti-miscegenation laws barring interracial marriage
- 1967: Loving v. Virginia strikes down bans on interracial marriage nationwide
- 1970s: No-fault divorce laws begin spreading state by state
- 1993-2000s: Varying state-level domestic partnership and civil union laws emerge
- 2015: Obergefell v. Hodges legalizes same-sex marriage nationwide
- International comparison point: Long-standing traditions of arranged marriage remain common today
- International comparison point: Multiple Indigenous nations historically recognized relationship
  and family structures (including gender-diverse roles) not reflected in Euro-American law until recently

Visual style: Horizontal timeline, color-coded by category (legal milestone = blue,
social/cultural shift = green, comparative note = gray)

Interactive features: click any event for an expanded infobox with a one-paragraph description and
why it matters for understanding that relationship norms are historically situated, not fixed

Implementation: vis-timeline with custom item colors and an onclick detail panel
```
