# Course Description — Grades 9-12

This is the Grades 9-12 section of the *Health Education* intelligent
textbook. It is one of 8 parallel grade-band sections (see the site-wide
[Course Description](../../course-description.md)); each band has its own
concept list, learning graph, chapters, quizzes, MicroSims, and infographics
at a matching reading level. Source: 2025 Minnesota K-12 Health Academic
Standards, Commissioner Approved (see [references.md](../../references.md)).
Benchmark codes below (e.g. `9.1.1.1`) are `<grade band>.<strand>.<anchor>.<seq>`.
This is the terminal band and a grade span (9-12), matching the highest
cognitive demand in the standards.

## Reading Level & Cognitive Demand

High school. Cognitive demand peaks here: **design, evaluate, synthesize,
apply across contexts**. Benchmarks require students to construct plans
(e.g., a personal nutrition plan), evaluate evidence (e.g., beverage choices
using hydration/sugar/caffeine data), and reason about systemic and cultural
factors (e.g., how income and food systems affect chronic-disease risk).
Appendix A of the source standards lists statewide-mandated topics that
culminate in this band: CPR/AED, vaping awareness/prevention, cannabis and
other substance use (including fentanyl), STI/HIV/HPV prevention, and
mental health education including suicide/self-harm prevention (see
[references.md](../../references.md)).

## Audience

Grade 9-12 students (age ~14-18) and their teachers. This is also the band
most directly relevant to career/college/community readiness framing per the
standards' introduction.

## Prerequisites

Grade 6-8 benchmarks across all 6 strands, including the 6-8 introduction of
Sexual Health and Substance Use Awareness and Prevention.

## Bridging Concepts for Students New to This Band

A student entering directly at Grade 9-12 without any earlier band should be
briefly introduced to these ideas first, since this band assumes them
without re-teaching:

- Reading nutrition labels and evaluating dietary guidelines across
  cultures; how food systems and income relate to health.
- Relationship structures vary across individuals and cultures; consent is
  central to healthy relationships; STIs/HIV exist, are transmitted in known
  ways, and prevention/treatment resources exist.
- Warning signs of a mental health crisis (including suicide/self-harm) can
  be recognized, and there are known ways to seek help for yourself or
  others; stigma around mental health can be actively reduced.
- Physical fitness has multiple components; CPR/AED and basic emergency
  response are learnable, teachable skills.
- Digital communication and social media carry safety, consent, and
  boundary implications; school-safety practices exist for emergencies.
- Substances (alcohol, tobacco, nicotine, cannabis, and others) carry
  physical, mental, and legal consequences; substance-use disorder,
  overdose, and recovery are real, addressable situations, not just
  cautionary abstractions.
- Health-related decisions and goals can be analyzed, planned, and
  evaluated using a structured process, not just instinct.

## Topics

### Food and Nutrition
- Designing a nutrition plan that meets personal needs and preferences
  (`9.1.1.1`)
- Using food labels to analyze nutrient values and their health impact
  (`9.1.1.2`)
- Evaluating personal beverage choices using hydration/sugar/caffeine
  evidence (`9.1.1.3`)
- Evaluating meals/snacks for daily life and physical activity using
  nutrition strategies (`9.1.1.4`)
- How cultural traditions, community initiatives, and food systems affect
  healthy diets and chronic-disease risk (`9.1.1.5`)

### Human Growth and Development and Sexual Health
- Characteristics of healthy relationships and handling conflict/pressure
  (`9.2.1.1`)
- How cultural, social, historical, and personal factors shape relationship
  structures (`9.2.1.2`)
- Showing dignity and compassion for all people across situations and
  relationships (`9.2.1.3`)
- How guidance from family, trusted adults, and culturally safe healthcare
  providers influences health/relationship decisions (`9.2.1.4`)
- Adolescents' rights to access healthcare services; what to expect from
  youth-friendly providers (`9.2.1.5`)
- How stereotypes, bias, myths, stigma, and prejudice affect relationships,
  behavior, health, and access to culturally safe healthcare (`9.2.1.6`)
- STI prevention, treatment, and health outcomes, using culturally safe and
  respectful approaches (`9.2.1.7`)
- The role of healthcare services and community-based programs in
  supporting healthy relationships/sexual health, and how culturally
  responsive services reduce barriers (`9.2.1.8`)
- Setting/communicating personal boundaries to support decision-making that
  prevents pregnancy and STIs, reflecting mutual respect (`9.2.1.9`)

### Mental and Emotional Health
- Deciding when/how to seek help for mental/emotional health concerns,
  including thoughts of suicide or self-harm (`9.3.1.1`)
- Causes, symptoms, and effects of depression and anxiety; treatment
  options (`9.3.1.2`)
- Evaluating mental health challenges and peer crises; supporting others
  while maintaining boundaries (`9.3.1.3`)
- How empathy and communication foster healthy relationships and nonviolent
  conflict resolution, including Indigenous restorative-justice approaches
  (`9.3.1.4`)
- Strategies for managing emotions and supporting intra/interpersonal
  wellbeing, drawing on cultural teachings (`9.3.1.5`)
- Designing strategies to foster a supportive, inclusive environment that
  addresses exclusion, bullying, stereotyping, prejudice, discrimination
  (`9.3.1.6`)
- Reducing stigma and increasing mental health awareness in schools and
  communities (`9.3.1.7`)
- Strategies to maintain brain health; Alzheimer's disease and other
  dementias; supporting an elder with cognitive impairment (`9.3.1.8`)

### Personal Health and Wellness
- How personal care practices (sleep, rest) influence lifelong health, and
  how culture/community/social conditions create health disparities
  (`9.4.1.1`)
- How access to physical activity differs across communities; safe
  participation practices; systemic inequities in health opportunity
  (`9.4.1.2`)
- Demonstrating CPR techniques and knowing when/how to use an AED
  (`9.4.1.3`)
- How behavioral, environmental, and lifestyle factors influence infectious
  and chronic disease, and strategies to prevent them (`9.4.1.4`)

### Personal Safety and Violence Prevention
- How digital content, social media, and online communication affect
  expectations, behaviors, and boundaries (`9.5.1.1`)
- Safe/appropriate actions for threats, harmful content, or online
  emergencies (`9.5.1.2`)
- Evaluating school safety practices/policies, including school shootings
  (`9.5.1.3`)
- Communicating, interpreting, and withdrawing consent; how power dynamics
  and law (federal, state, local, tribal) affect consent (`9.5.1.4`)

### Substance Use Awareness and Prevention
- How substance use affects mental health, brain function, and physiology,
  and disrupts relationships/school/work, considering Indigenous views of
  health (`9.6.1.1`)
- How substance use affects decision-making; coping strategies/alternatives
  drawing on cultural practices (`9.6.1.2`)
- Differences between commercial tobacco addiction and traditional
  tobacco/plant medicine use (`9.6.1.3`)
- Risks of mixing medications/substances; safe actions vs. traditional
  medicine use (`9.6.1.4`)
- Supporting yourself/others in preventing or reducing substance use,
  including cultural resilience strategies (`9.6.1.5`)
- Short- and long-term physical, mental, and relational effects of alcohol,
  tobacco, nicotine, cannabis, and other substances (`9.6.1.6`)
- Recognizing overdose signs and appropriate emergency response (`9.6.1.7`)
- How communities integrate cultural practices with prevention/treatment
  for resilience and recovery (`9.6.1.8`)
- Community laws, policies, and financial/social costs of substance use,
  including tribal sovereignty in lawmaking (`9.6.1.9`)
- How policy/regulation shapes marketing and availability of alcohol,
  tobacco, and other substances, and resulting health disparities
  (`9.6.1.10`)

## Learning Outcomes

Outcomes below are grouped by Bloom's Taxonomy level and traced to their
source benchmark codes. Grade 9-12 is heavily weighted toward Evaluate — the
standards repeatedly use "apply skill cues to evaluate" language at this
terminal band, reflecting career/college/community readiness.

### 1. Remember

By the end of Grade 12, the student will be able to:

1. Identify safe and appropriate actions to take in response to online
   threats, harmful content, or emergencies (`9.5.1.2`).
2. Recognize the signs of an overdose from medications or substances
   (`9.6.1.7`).

### 2. Understand

1. Explain the characteristics of healthy relationships and how to handle
   challenges, including conflict or pressure (`9.2.1.1`).
2. Describe how to show dignity and compassion for all people in a range
   of situations and relationships (`9.2.1.3`).
3. Identify what to expect from youth-friendly healthcare providers
   (`9.2.1.5`).
4. Summarize STI prevention, treatment, and health outcomes using culturally
   safe and respectful approaches (`9.2.1.7`).
5. Explain strategies to maintain brain health and describe Alzheimer's
   disease and other dementias (`9.3.1.8`).
6. Summarize the short- and long-term physical, mental, and relational
   effects of alcohol, commercial tobacco, nicotine, cannabis, and other
   substances (`9.6.1.6`).
7. Explain appropriate responses to a suspected overdose, including
   contacting emergency services (`9.6.1.7`).

### 3. Apply

1. Demonstrate how to support others through a mental health challenge or
   peer crisis while maintaining boundaries (`9.3.1.3`).
2. Demonstrate safe participation in physical activity, including proper
   equipment use, warm-up/cool-down routines, and hydration (`9.4.1.2`).
3. Demonstrate CPR techniques and know when and how to use an AED
   (`9.4.1.3`).
4. Apply safe and appropriate actions in response to online threats or
   emergencies (`9.5.1.2`).
5. Use an individual, supported, or collaborative goal-setting process as
   appropriate (`9.7.6.2`).
6. Adapt and demonstrate a variety of practices and behaviors that support
   health and wellbeing (`9.7.7.2`).

### 4. Analyze

1. Use food labels to analyze nutrient values and their potential health
   impact (`9.1.1.2`).
2. Examine how cultural traditions, community initiatives, and food systems
   contribute to healthy diets and chronic-disease risk (`9.1.1.5`).
3. Analyze how cultural, social, historical, and personal factors shape
   relationship structures (`9.2.1.2`).
4. Examine adolescents' rights to access a range of healthcare services
   (`9.2.1.5`).
5. Analyze how stereotypes, bias, myths, stigma, and prejudice affect
   relationships, behaviors, and health (`9.2.1.6`).
6. Analyze how setting and communicating personal boundaries supports
   decisions that prevent pregnancy and STIs (`9.2.1.9`).
7. Analyze how empathy and communication foster healthy relationships and
   nonviolent conflict resolution (`9.3.1.4`).
8. Analyze how access to physical activity differs across communities
   (`9.4.1.2`).
9. Analyze how substance use can affect decision-making (`9.6.1.2`).
10. Analyze ways to support yourself and others in preventing or reducing
    substance use (`9.6.1.5`).
11. Analyze how policies and regulations shape the marketing and
    availability of alcohol, tobacco, and other substances (`9.6.1.10`).
12. Analyze how public health policies, laws, and community/school
    environments influence health practices (`9.7.2.1`).
13. Analyze how culture, peers, perceived norms, attitudes, and beliefs
    influence health practices (`9.7.2.2`).
14. Analyze the accessibility and validity of trusted adults, health
    professionals, and other resources (`9.7.3.1`).
15. Analyze verbal and nonverbal communication skills across various types
    of communication (`9.7.4.1`).
16. Analyze conflict-resolution and negotiation skills for a variety of
    health-related situations (`9.7.4.2`).
17. Analyze how health-related decisions may affect personal and community
    wellbeing from a variety of perspectives (`9.7.5.1`).
18. Analyze a variety of options and potential consequences when making
    health-related decisions (`9.7.5.2`).
19. Examine factors affecting health promotion at individual through
    environmental levels (`9.7.8.1`).

### 5. Evaluate

1. Evaluate personal beverage choices using evidence about hydration,
   sugar, and caffeine content (`9.1.1.3`).
2. Evaluate meals and snacks for daily life and physical activity using
   nutrition strategies (`9.1.1.4`).
3. Evaluate how guidance from family, trusted adults, and culturally safe
   healthcare providers can influence health/relationship decisions
   (`9.2.1.4`).
4. Evaluate the role of healthcare services and community-based programs
   in supporting healthy relationships and sexual health (`9.2.1.8`).
5. Evaluate situations to decide when and how to seek help for mental or
   emotional health concerns, including thoughts of suicide or self-harm
   (`9.3.1.1`).
6. Analyze the causes, symptoms, and effects of depression and anxiety, and
   evaluate strategies and treatment options (`9.3.1.2`).
7. Evaluate mental health challenges and peer crises (`9.3.1.3`).
8. Evaluate strategies for managing emotions and their support for
   intrapersonal and interpersonal wellbeing (`9.3.1.5`).
9. Evaluate and develop strategies that reduce stigma and increase
   awareness of mental health in schools and communities (`9.3.1.7`).
10. Evaluate how personal care practices, including sleep and rest,
    influence lifelong health (`9.4.1.1`).
11. Evaluate how behavioral, environmental, and lifestyle factors influence
    infectious and chronic diseases, and assess prevention strategies
    (`9.4.1.4`).
12. Evaluate how digital content, social media, and online communication
    affect expectations, behaviors, and boundaries (`9.5.1.1`).
13. Evaluate school safety practices and policies related to emergencies,
    including school shootings (`9.5.1.3`).
14. Evaluate how to communicate, interpret, and withdraw consent, and how
    power dynamics and law affect consent (`9.5.1.4`).
15. Evaluate how substance use affects mental health, brain function, and
    overall physiological health (`9.6.1.1`).
16. Evaluate the differences between commercial tobacco addiction and the
    traditional use of tobacco and other plant medicines (`9.6.1.3`).
17. Evaluate strategies to prevent harmful interactions when mixing
    medications and other substances (`9.6.1.4`).
18. Evaluate how various communities integrate cultural practices with
    prevention and treatment approaches (`9.6.1.8`).
19. Evaluate community laws, policies, and the financial/social costs
    related to substance use (`9.6.1.9`).
20. Evaluate how substance-related policies influence individual choices,
    community health, and health disparities (`9.6.1.10`).
21. Evaluate how individual, interpersonal, community, societal, and
    environmental factors influence health practices (`9.7.2.3`).
22. Use and evaluate the validity, reliability, and accessibility of health
    information, products, services, and other resources (`9.7.3.2`).
23. Evaluate communication related to boundaries and expressing or
    withdrawing consent (`9.7.4.3`).
24. Evaluate refusal skills and strategies in a variety of health-related
    situations (`9.7.4.4`).
25. Choose the healthiest option, justify why it's the healthiest choice,
    and evaluate outcomes (`9.7.5.3`).
26. Evaluate personal health, wellbeing, and factors to justify focus areas
    for goal setting (`9.7.6.1`).
27. Evaluate supports and barriers to engaging in health-related practices
    (`9.7.7.1`).
28. Evaluate and explain the impact of practices and behaviors that support
    health and wellbeing (`9.7.7.2`).
29. Evaluate and refine the process, outcomes, and overall effectiveness of
    promoting health, safety, and wellbeing (`9.7.8.3`).

### 6. Create

1. Design a nutrition plan that meets personal needs and preferences
   (`9.1.1.1`).
2. Design and evaluate strategies to foster a supportive and inclusive
   environment that addresses exclusion, bullying, and discrimination
   (`9.3.1.6`).
3. Develop coping strategies or healthy alternatives when substance use
   affects decision-making, drawing on culturally grounded practices
   (`9.6.1.2`).
4. Formulate strategies to support achieving a personal health goal
   (`9.7.6.3`).
5. Formulate and justify a health-enhancing position or message about an
   issue, supported by facts and tailored to a variety of audiences
   (`9.7.8.2`).
