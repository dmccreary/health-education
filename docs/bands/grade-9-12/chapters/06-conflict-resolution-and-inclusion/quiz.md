#### 2. In the nonviolent conflict resolution process, why does the chapter distinguish a "position" from an "underlying need"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Positions and needs always describe exactly the same thing
2. Needs matter only in workplace conflicts, not personal ones
3. A position is always more important to satisfy than a need
4. Positions can appear to conflict while the underlying needs behind them are actually compatible and easier to satisfy simultaneously
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. The chapter's example of two students wanting a laptop "first" shows that stated positions can seem like a direct conflict, while the underlying needs, such as an approaching deadline versus an hour of free time, may be entirely compatible. Uncovering needs rather than stopping at positions is what makes many conflicts resolvable.

#### 3. How does the guiding question of an Indigenous restorative justice approach differ from that of a punitive justice model?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A restorative approach asks who was harmed and what they need, while a punitive model asks what rule was broken and what punishment fits
2. Both models ask exactly the same guiding question
3. A punitive model always involves more participants than a restorative one
4. A restorative approach never holds anyone accountable for harm caused
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The chapter explicitly contrasts these guiding questions: punitive models focus on the broken rule and matching punishment, while restorative models focus on who was harmed and what they need to feel whole again. Both models take wrongdoing seriously, including real accountability, but restorative accountability is defined as repairing relationship and community rather than serving a passive punishment.

#### 4. A school disciplinary policy is enforced more harshly against one group of students than another, even though no single staff member consciously intends to discriminate. What does the chapter call this?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A form of nonviolent conflict resolution
2. Individual discrimination, since it involves specific staff decisions
3. Systemic (institutional) discrimination, because the pattern is built into the policy itself and produces unequal outcomes regardless of individual intent
4. Cultural teaching for wellbeing
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The chapter defines systemic discrimination as patterns built into policies or institutions that produce unequal outcomes for a group regardless of any single individual's personal intent. Addressing this requires examining and changing the policy itself, not just addressing individual behavior, since no amount of individual goodwill fixes a rule that produces unequal outcomes by its own design.

#### 6. A student feels their heart racing and their breathing becoming shallow right before a stressful conversation. Which emotion management strategy from the chapter most directly targets this physical stress response?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Cognitive reframing
2. Paced breathing, which directly reduces the body's physiological stress response within minutes
3. Policy design
4. Talking circles
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Paced breathing, often with a longer exhale than inhale, directly reduces the body's physiological stress response, including elevated heart rate and shallow breathing, within minutes. Cognitive reframing addresses the interpretation of a situation rather than the immediate physical stress response.

#### 7. Two students disagree over how to divide work on a group project and have already generated several possible solutions together. According to the five-step nonviolent resolution process, what should happen next?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Immediately end the conversation once any solution is mentioned
2. Return to restating each person's original position without considering needs
3. Choose whichever solution was proposed first, regardless of fit

    The correct answer is **D**. The chapter's five-step process moves from generating multiple solutions to evaluating them against both people's needs, selecting the option that satisfies the most real needs on both sides rather than simply the first idea proposed.
