#### 3. Why does correcting a "perceived norm" (such as students overestimating how many peers vape) measurably reduce the target behavior?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because much youth behavior is driven by a desire to match a perceived norm rather than an actual preference for the behavior itself, so closing the gap between perceived and actual rates reduces the behavior
2. Because survey data has no real connection to individual behavior
3. Because students always ignore data about their peers' actual behavior
4. Because injunctive norms and descriptive norms are identical concepts with no distinction
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The chapter explains that teenagers routinely overestimate how many peers vape, drink, or are sexually active, and that correcting this misperception, a strategy called social norms marketing, measurably reduces the target behavior because much youth behavior is driven by a desire to match a perceived norm rather than genuine preference.
