#### 1. According to this chapter, what does the term "health" mean at the middle school level?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The ongoing, active management of physical, mental/emotional, and social well-being shaped by choices and environment
2. Simply not being sick or injured
3. Physical well-being only, since mental and social factors come later
4. A fixed state that does not change once you reach a certain age
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The chapter defines health as something you actively manage using tools like reading labels and evaluating sources, not just the absence of illness. #### 2. On a Nutrition Facts label, what does a %DV of 20% or more indicate for a nutrient?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The nutrient is considered low in the product
2. The nutrient is considered high in the product
3. The product exceeds the FDA's legal limit for that nutrient
4. The number only applies to added sugars
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. The chapter explains that %DV is based on a 2,000-calorie reference diet: 5% DV or less counts as low, and 20% DV or more counts as high. This rule applies to any nutrient listed on the label, not just added sugars, and it is not a legal limit but a way to judge amounts.

    **Concept Tested:** Food Label Analysis

#### 3. Why do USDA MyPlate, Canada's Food Guide, and traditional Indigenous food systems all look different from each other?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Only one of them is based on real nutritional science
2. They disagree about whether variety and limiting added sugar matter
3. They share the same core nutritional science but present it differently based on culture, geography, and food history
4. Indigenous food systems are outdated and being phased out
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. All three systems agree on the underlying nutritional science, such as favoring variety and whole foods, but they present that science differently based on locally available and culturally meaningful foods. None of the three is more "correct" than the others.

#### 4. A cereal box says "all natural" on the front. What does this chapter say that claim actually guarantees?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The product is low in added sugar
2. The product contains no artificial ingredients at all
3. The product meets official dietary guidelines
4. Very little, since the phrase has no strict legal definition for most foods
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. The chapter explains that "all natural" is only loosely and inconsistently enforced and has no strict legal definition for most foods. A product can be labeled "all natural" and still be high in sugar, sodium, or refined ingredients.

#### 5. Why does the chapter say caffeine can affect a middle schooler more strongly than an adult?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Adolescent nervous systems are still developing, so the same dose can produce more pronounced effects
2. Adolescents metabolize caffeine faster than adults do
3. Caffeine only affects people over the age of 18
4. Energy drinks contain a chemically different type of caffeine than coffee
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Because adolescent nervous systems are still developing, the same dose of caffeine can produce a more pronounced jittery or anxious feeling than it would in an adult. This is one reason the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends adolescents avoid energy drinks entirely.

    **Concept Tested:** Caffeinated And Energy Drink Effects

#### 6. How does food sovereignty differ from food security?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Food sovereignty is only about calorie counts, while food security is about food quality
2. Food security means having enough food, while food sovereignty means having control over one's own food system
3. The two terms mean the same thing and are used interchangeably
4. Food sovereignty applies only to Indigenous nations, while food security applies to everyone else
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. A community can have enough calories (food security) and still lack food sovereignty if it has no control over what kind of food is available or whether it reflects the community's own culture and land. Food sovereignty is about control and self-determination, not just having enough to eat.

    **Concept Tested:** Food Sovereignty

#### 7. Maria is deciding between a bottle of regular soda and a glass of water after basketball practice. Based on this chapter, which statement best supports choosing water as the default?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Soda replaces lost electrolytes faster than water can
2. Sports drinks and regular soda are chemically identical to water
3. Water has zero calories and no added sugar, while a single can of soda can contain close to the full daily added-sugar limit for teens
4. Water only matters if practice lasted more than two hours
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The chapter notes that a single 12-ounce can of regular soda has around 39 grams of added sugar, close to or above the full daily limit recommended for adolescents, while water has zero calories and zero added sugar. This makes water the better everyday default choice.

    **Concept Tested:** Water Versus Sugary Drinks

#### 8. A fruit snack package says "Made with Real Fruit," but the ingredient list shows fruit puree listed fifth, after sugar, corn syrup, and food coloring. What should a careful reader conclude?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The claim is illegal and the company should be reported
2. The ingredient list proves the claim is completely false
3. Ingredient order does not matter when judging a product
4. The claim is technically accurate but the product is not primarily made of fruit
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Ingredients are listed by weight from most to least, so an ingredient listed fifth makes up a small portion of the product. "Made with real fruit" can legally apply even when the actual fruit content is small, so the claim is technically true but creates a misleading impression.

    **Concept Tested:** Food Label Analysis

#### 10. A neighborhood has no full-service grocery store within a mile and low car access, but a community garden run by residents themselves has recently opened there. Which statement best analyzes this situation using the chapter's concepts?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The neighborhood is no longer a food desert, since any garden fully solves that problem
2. The garden is a community food access initiative that also reflects food sovereignty, since residents themselves control what is grown
3. Food deserts only exist in rural areas, so this description must be inaccurate
4. The garden addresses food sovereignty but has nothing to do with community food access
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Community gardens are a type of community food access initiative, and this one reflects food sovereignty because the initiative is led by the residents themselves deciding what to grow, rather than food being delivered by an outside organization. A single garden does not automatically erase all food desert conditions in a neighborhood.

    **Concept Tested:** Community Food Access Initiatives
