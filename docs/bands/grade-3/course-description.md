# Course Description — Grade 3

This is the Grade 3 section of the *Health Education* intelligent textbook.
It is one of 8 parallel grade-band sections (see the site-wide
[Course Description](../../course-description.md)); each band has its own
concept list, learning graph, chapters, quizzes, MicroSims, and infographics
at a matching reading level. Source: 2025 Minnesota K-12 Health Academic
Standards, Commissioner Approved (see [references.md](../../references.md)).
Benchmark codes below (e.g. `3.1.1.1`) are `<grade>.<strand>.<anchor>.<seq>`.

## Reading Level & Cognitive Demand

Elementary, independent readers. This band introduces the first
**evaluative** benchmarks (e.g., "evaluate personal behaviors to prevent
injuries") and the first explicit mention of puberty as a body-change topic,
alongside continued describe/explain work.

## Audience

Grade 3 students (age ~8-9) and their teachers.

## Prerequisites

Grade 2 benchmarks across all 5 content strands, plus the Grade 2 skill
benchmarks (explaining influences, using communication skills, describing
decision options, goal-setting with reflection).

## Bridging Concepts for Students New to This Band

A student arriving directly at Grade 3 without the K-2 sections should be
briefly introduced to these ideas first:

- Food groups and drink choices affect energy and growth; food safety
  practices exist for a reason.
- Peer and family relationships can be healthy or unhealthy, and personal
  space/boundaries matter.
- Emotions can be named, they influence behavior, and trusted adults or
  peers can help when someone is struggling.
- Hygiene and environmental awareness (sun, noise, chemicals, water) protect
  the body; regular physical activity supports health.
- Technology and touch both have safety rules, including saying no and
  knowing safe places/routines during emergencies.
- A personal health goal can be set, acted on, and reflected on.

## Topics

### Food and Nutrition
- Body signals for hunger and fullness (`3.1.1.1`)
- Carbohydrates, protein, and fats on nutrition labels and how they help the
  body grow (`3.1.1.2`)
- Why limiting caffeinated/sugary drinks matters; beverages that support
  energy, strength, and recovery for physical activity (`3.1.1.3`)
- Food safety practices and preparation methods that promote healthy eating
  (`3.1.1.4`)

### Human Growth and Development
- Roles of family members and peers in healthy relationships (`3.2.1.1`)
- Recognizing the stages of puberty and asking for help managing physical
  and emotional changes (`3.2.1.2`)

### Mental and Emotional Health
- Resilience and how personal choices affect mental/emotional wellbeing
  (`3.3.1.1`)
- Coping with challenging situations and who to talk to (`3.3.1.2`)
- Mental health challenges and their effects (`3.3.1.3`)
- Empathy and how it supports friendships and care for others (`3.3.1.4`)
- How emotions influence behavior, and how expressing emotions affects self
  and others (`3.3.1.5`)
- How teasing, exclusion, or bullying affects individuals and community;
  treating all people of all abilities with kindness and respect (`3.3.1.6`)

### Personal Health and Wellness
- How different cultures/communities use traditional knowledge to protect
  the body from the environment (`3.4.1.1`)
- Benefits of regular physical activity for health, mood, and learning;
  dressing appropriately for weather (`3.4.1.2`)
- How cultural traditions and community practices prevent germ spread and
  why they work (`3.4.1.3`)

### Personal Safety and Violence Prevention
- How shared messages, images, or videos affect self/others; risks of
  sharing personal information (`3.5.1.1`)
- Evaluating personal behaviors to prevent injuries across multiple
  environments (`3.5.1.2`)
- How practicing safety procedures prepares students/staff for real
  emergencies; identifying trusted adults who can help (`3.5.1.3`)
- The right to personal boundaries (`3.5.1.4`)

### Skill Standards
- Who can influence health practices and behaviors (`3.7.2.1`)
- Determining the validity and reliability of health information, products,
  services, and other resources (`3.7.3.1`)
- Verbal and nonverbal communication to express thoughts, feelings, wants,
  and needs, including in decision-making situations (`3.7.4.1`)
- Expressing thoughts/feelings/wants/needs using I-statements, with support
  (`3.7.4.2`)
- Identifying a personal health-related behavior to set a goal, and
  determining whether assistance/collaboration is needed (`3.7.6.1`)
- Examining practices and behaviors that support health and wellbeing
  (`3.7.7.1`)
- Identifying health topics relevant and meaningful to self/others
  (`3.7.8.1`)

## Key Vocabulary

hunger/fullness signal, carbohydrate, protein, fat, nutrition label,
puberty, resilience, empathy, mental health challenge, bullying, exclusion,
personal boundary, validity/reliability of information, I-statement,
injury prevention, safety procedure

## Learning Outcomes

Outcomes below are grouped by Bloom's Taxonomy level and traced to their
source benchmark codes. Grade 3 is the first band with genuine Evaluate-level
benchmarks.

### 1. Remember

By the end of Grade 3, the student will be able to:

1. Recall body signals for hunger and fullness (`3.1.1.1`).
2. Recognize the stages of puberty (`3.2.1.2`).
3. Recall ways to cope with challenging situations and who to talk to
   (`3.3.1.2`).
4. Recall who can influence health practices and behaviors (`3.7.2.1`).
5. Recall health topics relevant and meaningful to self and others
   (`3.7.8.1`).

### 2. Understand

1. Explain how carbohydrates, protein, and fats on nutrition labels help
   the body grow (`3.1.1.2`).
2. Describe why it's important to limit caffeinated and sugary drinks
   (`3.1.1.3`).
3. Describe food safety practices and preparation methods that promote
   healthy eating (`3.1.1.4`).
4. Describe the roles of family members and peers in healthy relationships
   (`3.2.1.1`).
5. Describe resilience and how personal choices impact mental/emotional
   wellbeing (`3.3.1.1`).
6. Describe mental health challenges and their effects (`3.3.1.3`).
7. Describe empathy and how it supports friendships and care for others
   (`3.3.1.4`).
8. Explain how emotions influence behavior, and how expressing emotions
   affects oneself and others (`3.3.1.5`).
9. Explain how teasing, exclusion, or bullying impacts wellbeing, and why
   treating all people with kindness and respect matters (`3.3.1.6`).
10. Describe how different cultures and communities use traditional
    knowledge to protect the body from the environment (`3.4.1.1`).
11. Explain the benefits of regular physical activity, including dressing
    appropriately for weather (`3.4.1.2`).
12. Explain how cultural traditions and community practices prevent the
    spread of germs and why these practices work (`3.4.1.3`).
13. Describe how shared messages, images, or videos affect self and others,
    and the risks of sharing personal information (`3.5.1.1`).
14. Explain how practicing safety procedures prepares students and staff to
    respond in real emergencies (`3.5.1.3`).
15. Describe the right to personal boundaries (`3.5.1.4`).

### 3. Apply

1. Demonstrate asking for help to manage physical and emotional changes
   during puberty (`3.2.1.2`).
2. Demonstrate verbal and nonverbal communication skills to express
   thoughts, feelings, wants, and needs (`3.7.4.1`).
3. Express thoughts, feelings, wants, and needs using I-statements, with
   support (`3.7.4.2`).

### 4. Analyze

1. Identify a personal health-related behavior to set a goal, and determine
   whether assistance or collaboration is needed (`3.7.6.1`).
2. Examine practices and behaviors that support health and wellbeing
   (`3.7.7.1`).

### 5. Evaluate

1. Evaluate personal behaviors to prevent injuries across multiple
   environments (`3.5.1.2`).
2. Determine the validity and reliability of health information, products,
   services, and other resources (`3.7.3.1`).

### 6. Create

Grade 3 benchmarks do not yet include a design/construct task. Setting a
goal and determining whether help is needed (`3.7.6.1`, listed under
Analyze above) is the closest precursor — true goal-plan construction
begins at Grade 4.
