# Quiz: Mental And Emotional Health

Test your understanding of resilience, coping, mental health challenges, empathy, and kindness for all abilities with these review questions.

---

#### 1. What is "resilience"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Never feeling sad, mad, or discouraged
2. The ability to recover from a hard day or disappointment and keep going in a healthy way
3. Winning every game or contest
4. Avoiding hard situations completely
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Resilience is the ability to recover from a hard day, a disappointment, or a difficult situation, and keep going in a healthy way. It does not mean never feeling sad or discouraged.

    **Concept Tested:** Resilience

#### 2. Which everyday choice is described as helping a person's mental and emotional wellbeing?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Skipping sleep as often as possible
2. Getting enough sleep
3. Avoiding all physical activity
4. Never talking about feelings
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Getting enough sleep improves mood, focus, and patience with others. The chapter describes several everyday choices, including sleep, activity, caring relationships, and talking about feelings, that support wellbeing over time.

#### 3. A student feels frustrated after a hard homework problem. Which is a healthy coping strategy?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Taking slow, deep breaths and counting to ten
2. Yelling at a family member
3. Ignoring the feeling completely and never mentioning it
4. Giving up on all homework forever
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Taking slow, deep breaths and counting to ten is one of the healthy coping strategies listed in the chapter for managing strong feelings during a difficult moment.

    **Concept Tested:** Coping With Challenges

#### 4. Which of these is an example of "who to talk to" about a hard feeling?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Ignoring the feeling until it goes away
2. A stranger online
3. No one, because feelings should stay private
4. A parent, teacher, school counselor, or school nurse
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Who to talk to refers to trusted people in a student's life — such as a parent or guardian, teacher, school counselor, or school nurse — who can help when a feeling is heavy or hard to carry alone.

#### 5. Which statement best describes a "mental health challenge"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Something a person chooses and can decide to stop having
2. An ongoing difficulty with thoughts, feelings, or behavior that can be helped by trusted adults or professionals
3. Something only a few people in the whole world ever experience
4. Something shameful that should never be mentioned
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. A mental health challenge is an ongoing difficulty with thoughts, feelings, or behavior that affects daily life, and it can be helped by talking with trusted adults or health professionals. It is common and not anyone's fault.

    **Concept Tested:** Mental Health Challenge

#### 6. A classmate seems tired and pulls away from friends for a while. What does this NOT mean, according to the chapter?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>

2. That patience and kindness could help
3. That the classmate is being dramatic on purpose
4. That a trusted adult could help figure out what's going on
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The chapter is clear that signs like pulling away from friends or seeming tired do not mean a person is being dramatic or choosing to act that way — they may be effects of a mental health challenge that deserve patience, not judgment.

    **Concept Tested:** Effects Of Mental Health Challenges

#### 7. What is "empathy"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The ability to notice, understand, and care about what another person is feeling
2. Feeling sorry for someone without trying to understand them
3. Only caring about your own feelings
4. Avoiding people who feel differently than you
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Empathy is the ability to notice, understand, and care about what another person is feeling, even when that feeling is different from what the student is feeling. It is different from just feeling sorry for someone.

    **Concept Tested:** Empathy

---

#### 8. A friend says, "I didn't make the team." Which response shows the most empathy?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. "That's too bad, anyway, guess what happened to me today?"
2. "You probably didn't try hard enough."
3. "I get it, no big deal, let's move on."
4. "That sounds really disappointing. Do you want to talk about it?"
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. An empathetic response notices and cares about the friend's feeling, like disappointment, and invites them to share more, rather than dismissing the feeling or making it about someone else.

    **Concept Tested:** Empathy In Friendships

#### 9. Which pair correctly matches an unhealthy expression of frustration with a healthy expression of the same feeling?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Unhealthy: "I feel frustrated because this is hard." Healthy: yelling and slamming things
2. Unhealthy: staying calm. Healthy: yelling
3. Unhealthy: yelling and slamming things. Healthy: "I feel frustrated because this is hard for me."
4. Both yelling and calm words are equally healthy
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Yelling or slamming things is an unhealthy way to express frustration, while calmly saying "I feel frustrated because this is hard for me" names the feeling clearly — a healthy expression of the same emotion.

    **Concept Tested:** Expressing Emotions

#### 10. A classmate uses a wheelchair to get around the classroom. What is the most respectful way to include them in a game?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Leave them out so they don't have to worry about it
2. Only speak to their helper or aide instead of them
3. Assume they don't want to play at all
4. Ask them how they'd like to be included, and choose a game area that works for everyone
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Kindness and respect for all abilities means asking a classmate how they'd like to be helped or included, instead of assuming, and choosing activities that work for everyone rather than leaving anyone out.

    **Concept Tested:** Kindness And Respect For All Abilities
