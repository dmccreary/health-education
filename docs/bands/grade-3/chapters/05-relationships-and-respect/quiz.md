# Quiz: Relationships And Respect

Test your understanding of family and peer roles, healthy relationships, bullying, exclusion, and personal boundaries with these review questions.

---

#### 1. "Showing a younger sibling how to ride a bike" is an example of which family member role?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Providing safety
2. Emotional support
3. Contributing
4. Teaching
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Teaching means showing someone how to do new things, from tying shoes to riding a bike. Showing a sibling how to ride a bike fits the teaching role described in the chapter.

    **Concept Tested:** Family Member Roles

---

#### 2. What is a "peer"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Someone close to a student's own age, like a classmate or teammate
2. A student's parent or guardian
3. A family pet
4. A teacher or school principal
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. A peer is someone close to a student's own age, such as a classmate, teammate, or neighborhood friend. Peers take on roles that shape how a friendship or classroom relationship feels.

    **Concept Tested:** Peer Roles In Relationships

---

#### 3. Which of these is a sign of a healthy relationship?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. One person always decides everything alone
2. Mistakes are never mentioned again
3. People never disagree about anything
4. Both people listen to each other, even when they disagree
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. In a healthy relationship, both people listen to each other, even when they disagree, and work through disagreements without hurting each other with unkind words or actions.

    **Concept Tested:** Healthy Relationships

---

#### 4. What is "bullying"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Repeated, unwanted, and harmful behavior toward someone, usually involving a power difference
2. Any single disagreement between two friends
3. A game played at recess
4. Choosing not to play a certain game
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Bullying is repeated, unwanted, and harmful behavior — physical, verbal, or online — by one person or group toward another, usually where there is a real or perceived difference in power.

    **Concept Tested:** Bullying

---

#### 5. Two friends argue once about which game to play, then make up and move on. Is this bullying?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Yes, any disagreement counts as bullying
2. Yes, because someone felt upset
3. No, because bullying is repeated and intentional, not a single disagreement
4. No, because friends can never disagree
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Bullying is repeated and intentional, with a power difference involved. Not every unkind moment is bullying — a single disagreement between friends who make up afterward is different from a repeated pattern of harm.

    **Concept Tested:** Bullying

---

#### 6. A group of classmates always saves seats so one particular classmate can never sit with them. What is this an example of?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A healthy relationship
2. Exclusion
3. Active listening
4. A personal boundary
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Exclusion is the act of deliberately leaving someone out of a group, activity, or conversation. Saving seats so a classmate can never sit with the group is a real example of exclusion given in the chapter.

    **Concept Tested:** Exclusion

---

#### 7. According to the chapter, who can be affected when bullying or exclusion happens in a classroom?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Only the person who is bullying or excluding someone
2. No one except the person who is bullied
3. Only the teacher
4. The person targeted, bystanders, and the whole classroom community
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. The effects of bullying and exclusion reach beyond the person targeted — bystanders can feel unsafe or guilty, and the whole classroom or community can become less trusting and less welcoming.

    **Concept Tested:** Effects Of Bullying And Exclusion

---

#### 8. Why does the chapter describe the effects of bullying as a "ripple effect" across a whole classroom?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because only water activities are affected
2. Because bullying only affects the exact moment it happens
3. Because the harm spreads outward, affecting the person targeted, bystanders, and the whole community's trust
4. Because ripples make a classroom quieter
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Like a ripple spreading outward from a center point, the effects of bullying and exclusion start with the person targeted but extend to bystanders and lower the trust and safety of the whole classroom community.

    **Concept Tested:** Effects Of Bullying And Exclusion

#### 9. What does the "right to personal boundaries" mean?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Only adults are allowed to say no to unwanted touch
2. Every person has the right to say what feels comfortable for their own body, space, and belongings
3. Boundaries only apply to strangers, never to family
4. A person must always explain why they set a boundary
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Every person has the right to decide what feels comfortable for their own body, space, and belongings, and to have that boundary respected by others, including family members, peers, and adults.

    **Concept Tested:** Right To Personal Boundaries

---

#### 10. A friend keeps tickling a student after being asked to stop. What is the best response?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Say clearly, "Please stop, I don't like that," and tell a trusted adult if it continues
2. Tickle the friend back even harder
3. Say nothing and hope it stops on its own
4. Stop being friends with them immediately without saying anything
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. A clear, calm sentence like "Please stop, I don't like that" is the strongest way to set a boundary. If the boundary is still not respected, telling a trusted adult is always the right next step.

    **Concept Tested:** Right To Personal Boundaries
