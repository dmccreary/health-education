# Quiz: Growth and Development Across Cultures

Test your understanding of conflict management, cultural respect, and puberty as a life stage with these review questions.

---

#### 1. What is conflict management?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The skill of handling a disagreement in a way that respects everyone and looks for a fair solution
2. A way to avoid ever having disagreements with anyone
3. A rule that says an adult must solve every disagreement
4. A game played during recess
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Conflict management is not about avoiding disagreements — it is about handling them respectfully so that everyone's feelings and needs are heard, instead of letting things turn into yelling or name-calling.

    **Concept Tested:** Conflict Management

---

#### 2. Which sentence is an example of an "I-statement"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. "You always ruin everything."
2. "Whatever, I don't care."
3. "I feel frustrated when the rules keep changing."
4. "Give it to me right now."
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. An I-statement describes how you feel without blaming the other person. Saying "I feel frustrated when the rules keep changing" explains a feeling calmly, which is a key part of managing conflict respectfully.

    **Concept Tested:** Conflict Management

---

#### 3. What is the first step in good conflict management?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Repeat your side louder and louder
2. Pause before reacting
3. Ask an adult to decide immediately
4. Walk away and never speak to the person again
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Good conflict management starts by pausing and taking a breath instead of reacting right away. This helps a person respond calmly instead of saying something they might regret.

    **Concept Tested:** Conflict Management

---

#### 4. What does it mean to respect cultural differences?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Recognizing that people have different customs and traditions and treating those differences with curiosity and respect
2. Ignoring differences between classmates so nobody feels different
3. Believing that one culture's way of doing things is the only correct way
4. Only being friends with people from the exact same background
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Respecting cultural differences means recognizing that people from different cultures may have different customs, communication styles, and family roles, and treating those differences with curiosity rather than judgment.

    **Concept Tested:** Respecting Cultural Differences

---

#### 5. A classmate's family solves disagreements with a quiet family talk instead of talking it out right away like your family does. What is the best way to think about this difference?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Your classmate's family must be doing something wrong
2. You should tell your classmate their way is strange
3. Only one family in the whole class can be correct
4. Neither way is wrong — different cultures can have different, valid approaches to conflict
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. What feels like "the right way" to handle a disagreement in one family or culture might look different in another, and neither is wrong. Understanding this helps you avoid misreading a classmate's different approach as rude or strange.

    **Concept Tested:** Respecting Cultural Differences

#### 6. What does it mean to describe puberty as a "life stage"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. It is a single event that happens on one specific day
2. It only happens to some people, not everyone
3. It is a recognized, normal period in every human life, like infancy or adulthood
4. It is something that stops a person from being the same person
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Puberty as a life stage means it is not a single event, but a normal period of life during which the body and emotions gradually shift toward adulthood, just like infancy or early childhood are recognized life stages.

    **Concept Tested:** Puberty As A Life Stage

---

#### 7. Around what ages can puberty normally begin?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Only at exactly age 10 for everyone
2. Roughly between ages 8 and 14
3. Only after age 18
4. Only before age 5
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Puberty can begin any time roughly between ages 8 and 14, and starting earlier or later than a classmate does not mean anything is wrong. Timing varies widely and normally from person to person.

    **Concept Tested:** Puberty As A Life Stage

#### 8. What is the Quinceañera, mentioned in this chapter as an example of puberty across cultures?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>

#### 9. Deshawn notices his body is changing earlier than most of his friends. He feels a little worried and unsure who to talk to. What is the best next step for Deshawn?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Keep it a secret from everyone forever
2. Only ask friends his own age for medical advice
3. Talk to a trusted adult, like a parent or school counselor
4. Assume something is wrong with him
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. A trusted adult, such as a parent, guardian, teacher, or school counselor, listens carefully, answers honestly, and helps without making a student feel embarrassed. Every question about growing up is valid, and timing differences are completely normal.

#### 10. Priya and a classmate cannot agree on how to split up a group project, and the disagreement keeps happening every day for a week. What would be the most appropriate next step?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Ask a trusted adult, like a teacher, to help them find a fair solution
2. Keep arguing about it every day without changing anything
3. Give up on the project completely
4. Refuse to speak to the classmate for the rest of the year
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Not every disagreement needs an adult, but some do — especially if the same conflict keeps repeating and the people involved cannot find a fair solution on their own. Asking a trusted adult for help is a smart, respectful choice in this situation.

    **Concept Tested:** Trusted Adult
