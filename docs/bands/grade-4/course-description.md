# Course Description — Grade 4

This is the Grade 4 section of the *Health Education* intelligent textbook.
It is one of 8 parallel grade-band sections (see the site-wide
[Course Description](../../course-description.md)); each band has its own
concept list, learning graph, chapters, quizzes, MicroSims, and infographics
at a matching reading level. Source: 2025 Minnesota K-12 Health Academic
Standards, Commissioner Approved (see [references.md](../../references.md)).
Benchmark codes below (e.g. `4.1.1.1`) are `<grade>.<strand>.<anchor>.<seq>`.

## Reading Level & Cognitive Demand

Upper elementary. Benchmarks lean into **plan, explain, analyze, and
demonstrate refusal skills** — the first appearance of refusal-skill
language and of constructing a position from research. Per state
requirements, mental health instruction becomes a required component
starting at this band (2026-2027 school year, per
[references.md](../../references.md)).

## Audience

Grade 4 students (age ~9-10) and their teachers.

## Prerequisites

Grade 3 benchmarks across all 5 content strands, plus the Grade 3 skill
benchmarks (identifying influences, judging information validity, using
I-statements, setting a goal with a help/no-help decision).

## Bridging Concepts for Students New to This Band

A student arriving directly at Grade 4 without the K-3 sections should be
briefly introduced to these ideas first:

- Nutrition labels list carbohydrates, protein, fat, and other nutrients;
  hydration and limiting sugary/caffeinated drinks matter.
- Puberty is a stage of physical/emotional change that different people
  experience at different times, and it's normal to ask for help with it.
- Emotions can be identified, they influence behavior, and mental health can
  be discussed openly with trusted adults.
- Regular physical activity and basic hygiene/illness-prevention practices
  support long-term health.
- Personal boundaries exist, sharing personal information online carries
  risk, and safety procedures/drills exist for real emergencies.
- Health information should be checked for validity/reliability before it's
  trusted, and personal health goals can be set and pursued with or without
  help.

## Topics

### Food and Nutrition
- Planning a balanced meal; how carbohydrates, protein, fats, vitamins,
  minerals, and water support the body; locating them on food labels
  (`4.1.1.1`)
- Benefits of drinking enough water and limiting caffeinated/sugary drinks
  (`4.1.1.2`)
- Effects of sugary and salty foods on health (`4.1.1.3`)
- Safety practices for keeping food at safe temperatures (`4.1.1.4`)

### Human Growth and Development
- Managing conflicts respectfully across individuals and cultures
  (`4.2.1.1`)
- Changes during puberty as a life stage observed across cultures, including
  Indigenous communities (`4.2.1.2`)

### Mental and Emotional Health *(required instruction begins this band)*
- How cultural practices and self-regulation benefit mental health
  (`4.3.1.1`)
- Cultural practices and various methods for self-regulation (`4.3.1.2`)
- How emotions affect behavior and strategies to manage them in healthy
  ways (`4.3.1.3`)
- Healthy/safe ways to respond to teasing, exclusion, disagreements,
  bullying, unfair treatment, bias, and prejudice — witnessed or
  experienced (`4.3.1.4`)
- Mental health can change over time like physical health; culturally
  appropriate and professional help is available (`4.3.1.5`)

### Personal Health and Wellness
- Common causes of allergic reactions and prevention/management strategies
  (`4.4.1.1`)
- Cultural traditions (dance, community sports) as physical activity and
  their benefits to health, mood, and learning (`4.4.1.2`)
- Infectious vs. non-infectious disease; how germs spread; prevention
  strategies, including cultural perspectives on wellness/caregiving
  (`4.4.1.3`)

### Personal Safety and Violence Prevention
- Harmful technology behavior and its impact on safety and mental health
  (`4.5.1.1`)
- Situational awareness, recognizing harmful situations and exiting safely,
  and setting boundaries, including cultural/community perspectives
  (`4.5.1.2`)
- Behaviors and warning signs that indicate someone/something is unsafe or
  dangerous (`4.5.1.3`)
- Recognizing pressure from others and practicing strategies to assert
  choices safely (`4.5.1.4`)

### Skill Standards
- How family, peers, groups, culture, and media/technology influence
  practices and behaviors (`4.7.2.1`)
- Characteristics of valid and reliable health information, products, and
  services (`4.7.3.1`)
- Demonstrating refusal skills for a variety of situations and knowing when
  help is needed (`4.7.4.1`)
- Developing a plan (actions, resources, progress tracking) toward a
  health-related goal, identifying supports and barriers (`4.7.6.1`)
- Demonstrating and tracking practices/behaviors that support health and
  wellbeing (`4.7.7.1`)
- Using research to construct a position on a relevant/meaningful health
  topic (`4.7.8.1`)

## Key Vocabulary

macronutrient, vitamin, mineral, food temperature safety, puberty, conflict
management, self-regulation, mental health, bias, prejudice, allergy,
infectious disease, non-infectious disease, situational awareness, warning
sign, peer pressure, refusal skill, valid/reliable information, goal plan

## Learning Outcomes

Outcomes below are grouped by Bloom's Taxonomy level and traced to their
source benchmark codes. Grade 4 is the first band with genuine Create-level
benchmarks (planning, developing, constructing).

### 1. Remember

By the end of Grade 4, the student will be able to:

1. Recall cultural practices and methods for self-regulation (`4.3.1.2`).
2. Recall common causes of allergic reactions (`4.4.1.1`).
3. Recall behaviors and warning signs that could indicate someone is unsafe
   or that a situation could become dangerous (`4.5.1.3`).

### 2. Understand

1. Explain how carbohydrates, protein, fats, vitamins, minerals, and water
   support the body (`4.1.1.1`).
2. Summarize the benefits of drinking enough water and limiting
   caffeinated/sugary drinks (`4.1.1.2`).
3. Describe the effects of sugary and salty foods on health (`4.1.1.3`).
4. Explain safety practices for keeping food at safe temperatures
   (`4.1.1.4`).
5. Explain how to manage conflicts respectfully across individuals and
   cultures (`4.2.1.1`).
6. Describe the changes that occur during puberty as a life stage observed
   across cultures, including Indigenous communities (`4.2.1.2`).
7. Explain how emotions affect behavior and identify strategies to manage
   them in healthy ways (`4.3.1.3`).
8. Describe that mental health, like physical health, can change over time,
   and that culturally appropriate and professional help is available
   (`4.3.1.5`).
9. Describe strategies to prevent or manage allergic reactions (`4.4.1.1`).
10. Explain how engaging in cultural traditions such as dance and community
    sports provides physical-activity benefits (`4.4.1.2`).
11. Explain the difference between infectious and non-infectious diseases
    and how prevention strategies reduce illness (`4.4.1.3`).
12. Explain how family, peers, groups, culture, and media/technology
    influence practices and behaviors (`4.7.2.1`).
13. Describe characteristics of valid and reliable health information,
    products, and services (`4.7.3.1`).

### 3. Apply

1. Demonstrate healthy and safe ways to respond when experiencing or
   witnessing teasing, exclusion, bullying, unfair treatment, bias, or
   prejudice (`4.3.1.4`).
2. Practice strategies to assert personal choices safely when pressured by
   others (`4.5.1.4`).
3. Demonstrate refusal skills in a variety of situations (`4.7.4.1`).
4. Demonstrate and track practices and behaviors that support health and
   wellbeing (`4.7.7.1`).

### 4. Analyze

1. Analyze examples of harmful behavior when using technology and explain
   the impact on safety and mental health (`4.5.1.1`).
2. Analyze strategies for developing situational awareness, recognizing
   harmful situations, safely exiting them, and setting boundaries
   (`4.5.1.2`).

### 5. Evaluate

1. Describe and evaluate how cultural practices and self-regulation benefit
   mental health (`4.3.1.1`).

### 6. Create

1. Plan a balanced meal using foods from different food groups and locate
   nutrients on food labels (`4.1.1.1`).
2. Develop a goal plan that includes actions, resources, and
   progress-tracking, and identify supports and barriers (`4.7.6.1`).
3. Use research to construct a position on a relevant, meaningful health
   topic (`4.7.8.1`).
