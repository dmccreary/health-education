# Quiz: Foundations And Trusted Adults

Test your understanding of health, family and peer influence, emotions, and trusted adults with these review questions.

---

#### 1. What are the three connected parts of health described in this chapter?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Physical, mental/emotional, and social
2. Physical, academic, and financial
3. Emotional, academic, and spiritual
4. Physical, social, and financial
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The chapter describes health as three connected parts: physical health (your body), mental/emotional health (how you think and feel), and social health (your relationships). These three parts constantly affect each other, so a struggle in one area often shows up in the others too.

    **Concept Tested:** Health

#### 2. Which of the following is an example of family influence on decisions?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. An advertisement for a new video game
2. A parent sets a regular bedtime for the household
3. A stranger's opinion on social media
4. A classmate dares you to skip homework
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Family influence comes from the beliefs, habits, and rules of the people you live with. A parent setting a bedtime is a direct example of family shaping a health-related choice, like how much sleep you get each night.

    **Concept Tested:** Family Influence On Decisions

#### 3. Which of these is one of the five qualities of a good trusted adult described in the chapter?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. They always agree with whatever you say
2. They only talk about serious topics
3. They listen carefully instead of dismissing your concern
4. They keep your secrets no matter what, even if you're in danger
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. A good trusted adult listens carefully instead of interrupting or dismissing a concern. Other qualities include keeping your trust (except when your safety is at risk), staying calm, helping you find a next step, and being available when you need them.

    **Concept Tested:** Trusted Adult

#### 4. Why does the chapter say a stressful argument with a friend (social health) might make it hard to sleep (physical health)?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because social health has nothing to do with physical health
2. Because the three parts of health work independently
3. Because sleep problems always come from eating too much sugar
4. Because the three parts of health are connected and can affect each other
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Health is not made of separate, unrelated pieces. The chapter explains that physical, mental/emotional, and social health constantly affect each other, so a social problem like an argument can easily spill over into physical effects like trouble sleeping.

    **Concept Tested:** Health

#### 5. How does peer influence generally differ from family influence, according to the chapter?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Peer influence comes from friends and classmates, while family influence comes from the people who raise you
2. Peer influence only affects food choices, while family influence affects everything else
3. Peer influence is always more powerful than family influence
4. Family influence disappears completely once you reach Grade 5
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Family influence comes from the people who raise and live with you, while peer influence comes from friends and classmates who are usually the same age. Both can be positive or negative depending on the specific habit or message.

    **Concept Tested:** Peer Influence On Decisions

#### 6. Why does the chapter say it's helpful to recognize your own emotional triggers?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. So you can avoid ever feeling emotions
2. So you can notice a feeling early and choose how to respond, rather than letting it decide for you
3. So you can blame others for your reactions
4. So you can prove your emotions are always correct
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Every emotion is normal, but the behavior that follows it can be shaped with practice. Recognizing your common triggers early gives you a chance to choose a calm response instead of letting the feeling make the decision for you.

    **Concept Tested:** Emotions Shape Behavior

#### 7. Why does the chapter describe telling a trusted adult about a friend's health concern as caring rather than betrayal?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because it gets the friend in trouble, which teaches them a lesson
2. Because friends should never keep any secrets from each other
3. Because it connects the friend to help they may not be able to get on their own
4. Because trusted adults will keep the information a total secret no matter what
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. A caring friend routes a real health concern to someone who can actually help, even if the friend asked for privacy. This isn't betrayal — it's one of the most caring things a friend can do, because it connects them to real support.

    **Concept Tested:** Health Concern Reporting

#### 8. Which sentence best demonstrates using an "I" statement to tell a trusted adult about a concern?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. "You never listen to me about anything."
2. "Everyone says I should be worried about this."
3. "Something is wrong, but I don't want to talk about it."
4. "I felt scared when I couldn't sleep for a few nights, and I wanted to ask you about it."
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. An "I" statement describes your own experience and feelings clearly, rather than hinting or blaming. This example states the concern directly and explains how it felt, which makes it much easier for a trusted adult to understand and help.

    **Concept Tested:** Telling A Trusted Adult

#### 9. Your friend tells you she hasn't slept well in two weeks because she's anxious about something at home, and asks you not to tell anyone. What is the strongest response?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Gently tell her this sounds like something a trusted adult could help with, then tell a trusted adult yourself
2. Say nothing, since she asked you to keep it private
3. Tell several other classmates so they can help watch out for her
4. Wait to see if she brings it up again before doing anything
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Ongoing sleep loss and anxiety are exactly the kind of health concern trusted adults are trained to help with. A caring friend tells the friend gently and still follows through with a trusted adult, rather than staying silent or spreading the information to classmates.

    **Concept Tested:** Health Concern Reporting

#### 10. Which scenario shows peer influence pulling a decision away from health?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A friend invites you to join a walking club
2. A classmate pressures you to skip homework to hang out
3. A friend group normalizes talking through disagreements calmly
4. A friend reminds you to pack a water bottle
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Peer influence isn't automatically good or bad. A classmate pressuring you to skip a responsibility, like homework, pulls a decision away from a healthy habit, while the other examples show peer influence supporting healthy choices.

    **Concept Tested:** Peer Influence On Decisions
