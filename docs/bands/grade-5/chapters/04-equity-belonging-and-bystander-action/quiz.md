# Quiz: Equity, Belonging, And Bystander Action

Test your understanding of equity, belonging, inclusion, and safe bystander action with these review questions.

---

#### 1. What is the definition of equity as described in the chapter?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Giving each person what they specifically need to have a fair chance at the same outcome
2. Giving everyone the exact same thing regardless of need
3. Giving the most resources to whoever asks first
4. Giving nothing extra to anyone, ever
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Equity means giving each person what they specifically need for a fair chance, unlike equality, which gives everyone the exact same thing. A student who needs a taller box to see over a fence gets one, so everyone ends up with a genuinely fair chance.

    **Concept Tested:** Equity

---

#### 2. What is a bystander?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The person who starts a conflict
2. A person who witnesses something happening to someone else without being the one doing it or the one it's happening to
3. The adult in charge of a classroom
4. The person being targeted by bullying
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. A bystander witnesses something, like teasing or a fight, happening to someone else. Bystanders usually outnumber the people directly involved, so what a bystander chooses to do next matters enormously.

    **Concept Tested:** Bystander

---

#### 3. What is the key difference between equality and equity?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Equality and equity mean exactly the same thing
2. Equity gives everyone the identical amount, while equality adjusts for need
3. Equality gives everyone the same thing; equity gives each person what they specifically need
4. Equity always gives less to people who need more
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Equality means everyone gets the same thing, which can still leave some people without a fair chance. Equity means everyone gets what they need, which is why "fair" doesn't always mean identical.

    **Concept Tested:** Equity

---

#### 4. Why is asking "would this person act the same way if no one outside their close friend group was watching?" a useful test for belonging?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because it tests how loud a group can be
2. Because it shows who has the most friends
3. Because it only matters for adults, not students
4. Because someone who has to hide or perform to be accepted isn't really experiencing belonging
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Belonging means feeling valued and accepted as your true self. If someone is hiding, shrinking, or performing to be accepted, belonging is missing for that person even if no one is being openly excluded.

    **Concept Tested:** Belonging

---

#### 5. Why does the chapter say a bystander should never physically try to break up a fight?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because physically intervening in a fight can get the bystander seriously hurt and rarely stops the fight
2. Because it is against the rules to care about a fight
3. Because bystanders are never able to help in any way
4. Because only adults are allowed to feel concerned about a fight
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. A bystander's own safety always comes first. Pulling people apart can get a bystander seriously hurt and rarely stops the fight anyway — getting an adult immediately is the safest, most powerful action instead.

    **Concept Tested:** Preventing Fighting And Violence

---

#### 6. Why is asking "would you like a hand, or have it?" a good example of ability-inclusive kindness?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because it wastes time that could be spent doing something else
2. Because it recognizes that not everyone needs or wants help in the same way, so it's better to ask than assume
3. Because it embarrasses the person being asked
4. Because assuming what someone needs is always faster and more respectful
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Ability-inclusive kindness means asking what someone needs instead of assuming. Different classmates need different kinds of support, so checking first respects their independence and treats them the same way you'd want to be treated.

    **Concept Tested:** Ability-Inclusive Kindness

---

#### 7. Which action best demonstrates "including and supporting others"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Ignoring a classmate who seems left out
2. Waiting for someone else to invite the classmate first
3. Inviting a classmate who is sitting alone to join your group
4. Telling the classmate they should have made friends sooner
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Including and supporting others is the action side of equity and belonging — actually inviting, making room, or checking in with someone, rather than waiting for someone else to act or ignoring the situation entirely.

    **Concept Tested:** Including And Supporting Others

---

#### 8. You witness a classmate being bullied but don't feel safe confronting the person directly. What's the best action?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Confront the person doing the bullying directly and loudly
2. Film the situation to show a trusted adult later
3. Do nothing since you can't say anything directly
4. Refuse to laugh, film, or share, then get a trusted adult safely
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. When it doesn't feel safe to say something directly, refusing to laugh, film, or share removes the audience the bullying is looking for, and getting a trusted adult safely is one of the most effective actions a bystander can take.

    **Concept Tested:** Bystander Action Against Bullying

---

#### 9. A fight seems about to start on the playground and a crowd is gathering with phones out. What's the safest response?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Immediately find and alert a supervisor or teacher, and step back from the crowd instead of joining it
2. Move closer to get a better view of what's happening
3. Record the situation to share with friends later
4. Cheer to see how the situation plays out
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Getting an adult immediately is the single most important action when a fight starts or seems about to start. Stepping back from the crowd, instead of joining or filming it, keeps everyone safer and doesn't make the situation worse.

    **Concept Tested:** Preventing Fighting And Violence

---

#### 10. Comparing "joins in," "passive watcher," and "upstander" bystander roles, what makes the upstander role different?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The upstander is simply the person who films the situation for proof
2. The upstander chooses to take safe, deliberate action to help, instead of encouraging the harm or silently watching
3. The upstander is the same as the person being targeted
4. The upstander always confronts the person causing harm directly
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Joining in encourages harmful behavior to continue, and a passive watcher can unintentionally signal approval by doing nothing. An upstander instead takes safe, deliberate action — which does not have to mean direct confrontation, since getting an adult counts too.

    **Concept Tested:** Bystander
