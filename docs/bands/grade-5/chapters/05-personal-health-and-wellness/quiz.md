#### 1. How many hours of sleep do most 10- and 11-year-olds need each night?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. 5 to 6 hours
2. 6 to 8 hours
3. 9 to 12 hours
4. 13 to 15 hours
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Most 10- and 11-year-olds need about 9 to 12 hours of sleep every night, which is more than most adults need. This sleep supports growth, learning, mood, and a strong immune system.

#### 2. Which of these is one of the four fitness components described in the chapter?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Reading speed
2. Memory strength
3. Typing speed
4. Flexibility
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. The four fitness components covered in the chapter are cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength, flexibility, and balance and coordination. Flexibility describes how far your joints and muscles can stretch and move.

#### 3. Why does the chapter compare sleep to "moving files from a messy desk into organized folders"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because sleep is when your brain sorts and strengthens what you learned that day
2. Because sleep erases everything you learned that day
3. Because sleep has nothing to do with memory
4. Because desks and folders are more important than sleep
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. While you sleep, your brain organizes and strengthens memories and skills from the day, similar to organizing files into folders. This is one reason getting enough sleep supports learning.

    **Concept Tested:** Sleep Supports Growth And Learning

#### 4. Why is a chronic condition different from a common illness like a cold?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because a chronic condition is not a real health issue
2. Because a chronic condition lasts a long time and needs ongoing management, rather than a one-time cure
3. Because a chronic condition always goes away within a few days
4. Because a chronic condition only affects adults
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. A chronic condition, like asthma or type 1 diabetes, lasts a long time and needs an ongoing management plan. This is the key difference from a common illness like a cold, which usually clears up on its own within a short time.

    **Concept Tested:** Chronic Condition

#### 5. Why should you never wait to see if an allergic reaction is "probably nothing"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because waiting always makes symptoms go away faster
2. Because trusted adults are too busy to be told about mild symptoms
3. Because reactions can escalate in minutes, so getting help right away is always the right first move
4. Because only severe reactions are worth mentioning to anyone
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Allergic reactions can escalate from mild to severe in minutes. The single most important rule is to never wait and see — telling a trusted adult immediately is always the right first move, even for symptoms that seem minor.

    **Concept Tested:** Responding To Allergic Reactions

#### 6. Why does the chapter say personal behaviors add up to shape long-term health, rather than one single decision mattering most?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because only one perfect decision determines your entire health
2. Because personal behaviors have no real effect on long-term health
3. Because long-term health is decided entirely by genetics
4. Because everyday habits, practiced repeatedly over time, are what shape health far into the future
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"

#### 7. Which activity would best build cardiovascular endurance?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Running or swimming laps
2. Stretching before bed
3. Balance-beam walking
4. Push-ups
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Cardiovascular endurance is how well your heart and lungs supply oxygen during sustained activity, like running or swimming. Stretching mainly builds flexibility, balance-beam walking builds balance and coordination, and push-ups build muscular strength.

    **Concept Tested:** Physical Activities By Fitness Component

#### 8. A classmate has hives and a stuffy nose after eating peanuts, but is breathing fine. What is the best first response?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Wait to see if the hives go away on their own
2. Tell a trusted adult right away, help them avoid more contact with the allergen, and watch closely
3. Give them more peanuts to see if the reaction gets worse
4. Ignore it since they are still breathing normally
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. For a mild reaction like hives without breathing trouble, the correct response is to tell a trusted adult right away, help the person avoid more contact with the allergen, and keep watching closely, since mild symptoms can turn serious quickly.

    **Concept Tested:** Allergic Reaction Signs

---

#### 9. A friend has a fever that has lasted three days and keeps getting worse. What should you do?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Tell them to just rest and it will go away on its own
2. Wait another few days to see if it improves
3. Tell a trusted adult so the friend can be seen by a doctor soon
4. Suggest they skip telling anyone since a fever isn't a big deal
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. A fever that lasts more than a day or two, or symptoms that keep getting worse instead of better, are signs that a trusted adult and possibly a doctor should be involved soon, rather than just waiting it out.

    **Concept Tested:** Knowing When To Seek Help

#### 10. Comparing a mild allergic reaction to a severe one (anaphylaxis), what distinguishes signs that require calling emergency help immediately?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Any hives at all mean emergency help is needed
2. Mild itching always requires calling 911
3. There is no real difference between mild and severe reactions
4. Signs like facial or throat swelling, real trouble breathing, or fainting signal anaphylaxis, a life-threatening reaction
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Mild signs like a few hives or an itchy mouth are different from emergency signs like facial or throat swelling, real trouble breathing, or fainting, which signal anaphylaxis. Emergency signs mean calling for help immediately, not waiting to see what happens next.

    **Concept Tested:** Allergic Reaction Signs
