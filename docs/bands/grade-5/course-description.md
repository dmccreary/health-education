# Course Description — Grade 5

This is the Grade 5 section of the *Health Education* intelligent textbook.
It is one of 8 parallel grade-band sections (see the site-wide
[Course Description](../../course-description.md)); each band has its own
concept list, learning graph, chapters, quizzes, MicroSims, and infographics
at a matching reading level. Source: 2025 Minnesota K-12 Health Academic
Standards, Commissioner Approved (see [references.md](../../references.md)).
Benchmark codes below (e.g. `5.1.1.1`) are `<grade>.<strand>.<anchor>.<seq>`.
This is the last individually-graded band before the standards shift to
grade bands (6-8, 9-12).

## Reading Level & Cognitive Demand

Upper elementary, transitioning toward middle-school-style comparison and
application. Benchmarks emphasize **compare, use, apply, evaluate, and
demonstrate** — e.g., comparing nutrition labels between two foods,
evaluating strategies for recognizing unhealthy relationships.

## Audience

Grade 5 students (age ~10-11) and their teachers.

## Prerequisites

Grade 4 benchmarks across all 5 content strands, plus the Grade 4 skill
benchmarks (explaining influences, describing information validity,
demonstrating refusal skills, goal planning with barriers/supports).

## Bridging Concepts for Students New to This Band

A student arriving directly at Grade 5 without the K-4 sections should be
briefly introduced to these ideas first:

- Nutrition labels and food groups guide meal choices; caffeinated/sugary
  drinks and unsafe food handling carry health risks.
- Puberty involves physical/emotional change, and family/peer relationships
  influence feelings, behavior, and decisions.
- Mental health can be supported through self-regulation and cultural
  practices, and it's normal and healthy to ask a trusted adult for help.
- Bullying, exclusion, and unfair treatment harm mental health and belonging
  regardless of ability.
- Regular physical activity, allergy awareness, and disease-prevention
  habits (hygiene, safe food/water) support long-term health.
- Refusal skills, setting/respecting boundaries, and recognizing warning
  signs of unsafe situations are learnable skills, not just instincts.
- A health-related goal can be planned with actions, resources, and known
  barriers, then tracked over time.

## Topics

### Food and Nutrition
- Comparing cultural approaches to meeting daily nutrient/energy needs
  (`5.1.1.1`)
- Using nutrition labels to compare two similar foods and choose the
  healthier one (`5.1.1.2`)
- Comparing water to other drinks and how choosing water supports health
  (`5.1.1.3`)
- Applying nutritious eating strategies to plan and choose meals/snacks
  (`5.1.1.4`)
- How unsafe food practices cause foodborne illness (`5.1.1.5`)

### Human Growth and Development
- How family and peer relationships influence feelings, behavior, and
  decision-making (`5.2.1.1`)
- Effectively telling a trusted adult about a health concern or question,
  for yourself or someone else (`5.2.1.2`)

### Mental and Emotional Health
- Healthy/restorative strategies for managing personal growth and
  relationships (`5.3.1.1`)
- Supporting a peer in a difficult situation while respecting boundaries and
  guiding them to seek help (`5.3.1.2`)
- Including and supporting others to promote equity and belonging
  (`5.3.1.3`)
- Situations, people, and experiences that influence emotions, and how
  emotions influence behavior (`5.3.1.4`)
- How individual actions and bystander behaviors can prevent or reduce
  bullying, fighting, and violence (`5.3.1.5`)
- How treating people of all abilities with kindness supports an inclusive,
  welcoming environment (`5.3.1.6`)

### Personal Health and Wellness
- Why adequate sleep and rest matter for growth, learning, and wellbeing
  (`5.4.1.1`)
- How different physical activities support different fitness components;
  identifying one activity per component (`5.4.1.2`)
- Signs of allergic reactions and how to respond (`5.4.1.3`)
- Symptoms of common illnesses/chronic conditions, when to seek help, and
  how personal behaviors affect long-term health (`5.4.1.4`)

### Personal Safety and Violence Prevention
- How technology and social media can cause harm; prevention/reporting
  strategies (`5.5.1.1`)
- Recognizing unhealthy/harmful relationships, setting boundaries, seeking
  support, and safely exiting when needed (`5.5.1.2`)
- Individual, peer, and school-staff actions that reduce risk during an
  emergency (`5.5.1.3`)
- The difference between consent and coercion; situations that feel unsafe
  and how to report to a trusted adult (`5.5.1.4`)

### Skill Standards
- Strategies and resources to manage influences that impact health
  practices/behaviors (`5.7.2.1`)
- Determining which trusted adults/individuals/resources are needed for
  various situations (`5.7.3.1`)
- Demonstrating conflict-resolution skills to prevent/manage/resolve
  conflict (`5.7.4.1`)
- Demonstrating boundary-setting skills for self and others (`5.7.4.2`)
- Examining options and consequences affecting health-related outcomes
  (`5.7.5.1`)
- Reflecting on the results and impact of a health-related decision
  (`5.7.5.2`)
- Tracking progress and reflecting on a personal health goal (`5.7.6.1`)
- Analyzing the impact of health practices/behaviors and taking ownership of
  personal health (`5.7.7.1`)
- Delivering messages to various audiences and examining outcomes
  (`5.7.8.1`)

## Key Vocabulary

nutrition label comparison, foodborne illness, bystander, equity,
belonging, fitness component, allergic reaction, chronic condition,
social media harm, consent, coercion, boundary-setting, conflict
resolution, decision reflection

## Learning Outcomes

Outcomes below are grouped by Bloom's Taxonomy level and traced to their
source benchmark codes.

### 1. Remember

By the end of Grade 5, the student will be able to:

1. Recognize signs of allergic reactions (`5.4.1.3`).

### 2. Understand

1. Explain how unsafe food practices can cause foodborne illness
   (`5.1.1.5`).
2. Explain how family and peer relationships influence feelings, behavior,
   and decision-making (`5.2.1.1`).
3. Describe healthy, restorative strategies for managing personal growth
   and relationships (`5.3.1.1`).
4. Identify situations, people, and experiences that influence emotions,
   and explain how emotions influence behavior (`5.3.1.4`).
5. Explain how treating people of all abilities with kindness supports an
   inclusive, welcoming environment (`5.3.1.6`).
6. Explain why adequate sleep and rest are important for healthy growth and
   learning (`5.4.1.1`).
7. Describe how different types of physical activity support different
   fitness components (`5.4.1.2`).
8. Describe symptoms of common illnesses and chronic conditions and explain
   when to seek help (`5.4.1.4`).
9. Explain individual, peer, and school-staff actions that reduce risk
   during an emergency (`5.5.1.3`).
10. Explain the difference between consent and coercion (`5.5.1.4`).

### 3. Apply

1. Demonstrate how to effectively tell a trusted adult about a health
   concern or question (`5.2.1.2`).
2. Demonstrate supporting a peer in a difficult situation while respecting
   boundaries and guiding them to seek help (`5.3.1.2`).
3. Demonstrate ways to include and support others to promote equity and
   belonging (`5.3.1.3`).
4. Respond appropriately to a recognized allergic reaction (`5.4.1.3`).
5. Demonstrate conflict-resolution skills to prevent, manage, or resolve
   conflict (`5.7.4.1`).
6. Demonstrate boundary-setting skills for self and others (`5.7.4.2`).
7. Deliver a message to a chosen audience about a health topic (`5.7.8.1`).

### 4. Analyze

1. Compare cultural approaches to meeting daily nutrient and energy needs
   (`5.1.1.1`).
2. Use nutrition labels to compare two similar foods and determine which is
   healthier (`5.1.1.2`).
3. Compare water to other drinks and explain how choosing water supports
   health (`5.1.1.3`).
4. Analyze how individual actions and bystander behaviors can prevent or
   reduce bullying, fighting, and violence (`5.3.1.5`).
5. Identify strategies and resources to manage influences that impact
   health practices and behaviors (`5.7.2.1`).
6. Examine options and consider consequences that may affect health-related
   outcomes (`5.7.5.1`).
7. Analyze the impact of health practices and behaviors and explain the
   importance of taking ownership of personal health (`5.7.7.1`).

### 5. Evaluate

1. Evaluate ways technology and social media can cause harm and identify
   strategies for prevention and reporting (`5.5.1.1`).
2. Evaluate strategies for recognizing unhealthy or harmful relationships,
   setting boundaries, seeking support, and safely exiting when needed
   (`5.5.1.2`).
3. Determine which trusted adults, other individuals, and health resources
   are needed for various situations (`5.7.3.1`).
4. Reflect on the results of a health-related decision and its impact
   (`5.7.5.2`).
5. Track progress and reflect on achieving a personal health goal
   (`5.7.6.1`).

### 6. Create

1. Apply nutritious eating strategies to plan and choose meals and snacks
   (`5.1.1.4`).
