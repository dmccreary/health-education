# Course Description — Grade 1

This is the Grade 1 section of the *Health Education* intelligent textbook.
It is one of 8 parallel grade-band sections (see the site-wide
[Course Description](../../course-description.md)); each band has its own
concept list, learning graph, chapters, quizzes, MicroSims, and infographics
at a matching reading level. Source: 2025 Minnesota K-12 Health Academic
Standards, Commissioner Approved (see [references.md](../../references.md)).
Benchmark codes below (e.g. `1.1.1.1`) are `<grade>.<strand>.<anchor>.<seq>`.

## Reading Level & Cognitive Demand

Early elementary (beginning independent readers, with read-aloud support
still expected). Benchmarks shift from Kindergarten's pure identify/name
toward **identify and explain why**, and toward simple **demonstration**
(e.g., saying "no" to unwanted touch).

## Audience

Grade 1 students (age ~6-7) and their teachers. Short sentences, high
picture-to-text ratio; independent reading is emerging but not assumed for
comprehension of nuance.

## Prerequisites

Kindergarten benchmarks in the same 5 content strands (food groups,
recognizing feelings, daily personal-care habits, safety rules, correct body
part names) and the Kindergarten skill benchmarks (identifying trusted
adults, recognizing influences, naming a personal goal).

## Bridging Concepts for Students New to This Band

A student arriving directly at Grade 1 without the Kindergarten section
should be briefly introduced to these ideas before Grade 1 content, since
Grade 1 benchmarks build on them without re-teaching them:

- What a food group is, and that food/water keep the body running.
- Feelings have names, and it's healthy to talk about them.
- A trusted adult is someone at home, school, or in the community you can
  ask for help.
- Basic hygiene actions (handwashing, covering coughs) stop germs from
  spreading.
- Every body part has a correct name, and your body belongs to you.
- Simple safety rules exist at home, school, and in the community, and
  there are safe places to go in an emergency.

## Topics

### Food and Nutrition
- Why eating a variety of food from all food groups matters (`1.1.1.1`)
- Different drink types and their benefits (`1.1.1.2`)
- Why breakfast is important (`1.1.1.3`)
- Safe practices for storing, preparing, and handling food (`1.1.1.4`)

### Human Growth and Development
- Characteristics of healthy peer relationships (`1.2.1.1`)

### Mental and Emotional Health
- Identifying feelings and why talking about them matters, while honoring
  cultural practices for support (`1.3.1.1`)
- Common signs someone may be struggling emotionally (`1.3.1.2`)
- How valuing your own and others' uniqueness strengthens caring
  relationships (`1.3.1.3`)
- How different situations affect the way people feel and respond
  (`1.3.1.4`)
- How kindness and unkindness affect relationships and belonging (`1.3.1.5`)

### Personal Health and Wellness
- How daily personal-care practices, cultural traditions, and community
  practices support health (`1.4.1.1`)
- Benefits of regular physical activity for health, learning, and emotions;
  enjoyable ways to be active daily (`1.4.1.2`)
- Common ways germs spread and simple actions to prevent illness (`1.4.1.3`)

### Personal Safety and Violence Prevention
- Using technology safely and respectfully, including asking permission to
  use devices/share information (`1.5.1.1`)
- Safe and unsafe behaviors at home, school, and in the community, and
  knowing when to ask a trusted adult for help (`1.5.1.2`)
- Types of school emergencies and how safety drills keep everyone safe
  (`1.5.1.3`)
- Keeping one's body safe; saying "no" to unwanted touch (`1.5.1.4`)

### Skill Standards
- How various influences affect health practices and behaviors (`1.7.2.1`)
- When and how to seek help from others at home, school, or in the
  community (`1.7.3.1`)
- Recognizing and naming one's own and others' feelings; communicating
  thoughts, feelings, wants, and needs in healthy ways (`1.7.4.1`)
- Recognizing when help is needed and who can support a health-related
  decision (`1.7.5.1`)
- Demonstrating and tracking practices/behaviors that support health and
  wellbeing (`1.7.7.1`)
- Ways to ask for help from others in making safe, health-promoting choices
  (`1.7.8.1`)

## Key Vocabulary

food group, breakfast, food safety, peer relationship, uniqueness,
belonging, hygiene, germs, physical activity, permission, unsafe touch,
safety drill, influence, communication, health decision

## Learning Outcomes

Outcomes below are grouped by Bloom's Taxonomy level and traced to their
source benchmark codes.

### 1. Remember

By the end of Grade 1, the student will be able to:

1. Recall different drink types and the benefits they provide (`1.1.1.2`).
2. Recall safe practices for storing, preparing, and handling food
   (`1.1.1.4`).
3. Recall common signs that someone may be struggling emotionally
   (`1.3.1.2`).
4. Recall the benefits of regular physical activity for health, learning,
   and emotions (`1.4.1.2`).
5. Recall common ways germs spread (`1.4.1.3`).
6. Recall safe and unsafe behaviors at home, school, and in the community
   (`1.5.1.2`).
7. Recall different types of school emergencies (`1.5.1.3`).
8. Recall the ways various influences affect health practices and behaviors
   (`1.7.2.1`).
9. Recall when and how to seek help from others at home, school, or in the
   community (`1.7.3.1`).
10. Recall ways to ask for help from others in making safe health-promoting
    choices (`1.7.8.1`).

### 2. Understand

1. Explain why eating a variety of food from all food groups is important
   (`1.1.1.1`).
2. Explain why eating breakfast matters (`1.1.1.3`).
3. Describe characteristics of healthy peer relationships (`1.2.1.1`).
4. Explain why talking about feelings is important, while honoring cultural
   practices for support (`1.3.1.1`).
5. Describe how valuing your own and others' uniqueness strengthens caring
   relationships (`1.3.1.3`).
6. Describe how different situations can affect the way people feel and
   respond (`1.3.1.4`).
7. Explain how kindness and unkindness affect relationships and belonging
   (`1.3.1.5`).
8. Describe how daily personal-care practices, cultural traditions, and
   community practices contribute to health and wellbeing (`1.4.1.1`).
9. Describe simple actions that prevent illness (`1.4.1.3`).
10. Explain how to use technology safely and respectfully, including asking
    permission before using devices or sharing information (`1.5.1.1`).
11. Explain how safety drills help keep everyone safe during an emergency
    (`1.5.1.3`).
12. Describe ways to keep one's body safe (`1.5.1.4`).
13. Recognize when help is needed and who can support a health-related
    decision (`1.7.5.1`).

### 3. Apply

1. Demonstrate saying "no" to unwanted touch (`1.5.1.4`).
2. Demonstrate communicating thoughts, feelings, wants, and needs in
   healthy ways (`1.7.4.1`).
3. Demonstrate and track practices and behaviors that support health and
   wellbeing (`1.7.7.1`).

### 4. Analyze

1. Distinguish safe from unsafe behaviors at home, school, and in the
   community (`1.5.1.2`).
2. Distinguish which people or situations positively or negatively
   influence a health practice or behavior (`1.7.2.1`).

### 5. Evaluate

Grade 1 benchmarks do not yet include a compare-options or judge-evidence
task. The closest emerging skill is recognizing when a situation calls for
outside help (`1.7.5.1`, listed under Understand above). True evaluative
reasoning (comparing choices using evidence) begins at Grade 2-3.

### 6. Create

Grade 1 benchmarks do not yet include a design/construct task. Tracking a
practiced behavior over time (`1.7.7.1`, listed under Apply above) is the
closest precursor to the goal-design outcomes that begin at Grade 2.
