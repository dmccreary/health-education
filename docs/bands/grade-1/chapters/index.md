# Grade 1 Health Education — Chapters

This section contains 7 chapters that build on each other in order. Each
chapter introduces a small set of related ideas and assumes students have
already read the chapters before it. Teachers and families can read a
chapter aloud together, talk through the pictures and examples, and revisit
the "Concepts Covered" list at the end to check understanding before moving
on to the next chapter.

1. [Foundations of Health and Safe Habits](01-foundations/index.md) —
   Introduces health and the core ideas of trusted adults, permission, safe
   behavior, influence, and communication that every later chapter builds on.
2. [Food, Drinks, and Staying Safe in the Kitchen](02-food-and-nutrition/index.md) —
   Explores food groups, drink choices, why breakfast matters, and safe food
   storage and preparation.
3. [Understanding and Talking About Feelings](03-feelings/index.md) —
   Helps students name feelings, talk about them, and notice how situations
   and struggles affect emotions.
4. [Friendship, Uniqueness, and Belonging](04-friendship-and-belonging/index.md) —
   Looks at healthy peer relationships, valuing uniqueness, kindness, and
   belonging.
5. [Healthy Habits, Activity, and Germs](05-healthy-habits/index.md) —
   Covers hygiene, personal care, physical activity, and how germs spread
   and can be prevented.
6. [Staying Safe at Home, School, and Online](06-personal-safety/index.md) —
   Teaches safe technology use, telling safe from unsafe behavior, emergency
   drills, body safety, and asking a trusted adult for help.
7. [Getting Help and Making Healthy Choices](07-help-and-decisions/index.md) —
   Brings together influence, communication, and help-seeking skills to
   support making and tracking healthy decisions.

## How to Use This Textbook

Read the chapters in order, since each one uses ideas from the chapters
before it. Start with the Summary to see what the chapter is about, then
review the Concepts Covered list together, using the pictures and
read-aloud text to talk through new words. There is no need to memorize
definitions — the goal is for students to recognize each idea, explain it
in their own words, and, where noted, practice it (like saying "no" to an
unwanted touch) with the support of a trusted adult or teacher.
