1. **Medical Treatment** — Healthcare can address withdrawal, cravings, co-occurring conditions, and overall health.
2. **Counseling** — Counseling can build coping, insight, skills, and a plan for difficult moments.
3. **Peer Support** — People with lived experience can offer understanding, hope, and practical wisdom.
4. **Family and Chosen Family** — Supportive relationships can provide stability, encouragement, and accountability.
5. **Culture and Community** — Cultural identity, ceremony, faith, language, and community can strengthen belonging.
6. **School and Work** — Flexible, supportive school or work settings can restore routine and purpose.
7. **Healthy Coping** — Sleep, movement, creativity, connection, and other coping skills support wellbeing.
8. **Safe Housing** — Stable housing can make treatment, sleep, safety, and routines more possible.
9. **Supportive Policy** — Policies affect access, stigma, treatment, housing, school, and employment.
10. **Relapse Response** — A return to use signals a need to reassess safety and support, not moral failure.
11. **Renewed Goals** — Recovery can reconnect a person with relationships, learning, creativity, and purpose.
12. **Long-Term Recovery** — Recovery can be an ongoing process with changing needs over time.
