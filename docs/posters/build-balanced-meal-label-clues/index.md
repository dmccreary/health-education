1. **Start with the Serving Information** — On a real package, serving information tells what amount the rest of the label describes.
2. **Whole-Grain Carbohydrate** — Whole-grain foods can provide carbohydrates for energy along with fiber.
3. **Colorful Produce** — Fruits and vegetables can contribute fiber, vitamins, minerals, color, and texture.
4. **Beans and Lentils** — Beans and lentils can contribute protein, carbohydrate, fiber, vitamins, and minerals.
5. **Calcium-Rich Choice** — Dairy foods and fortified alternatives can contribute calcium and other nutrients.
6. **Fats with Useful Jobs** — Nuts, seeds, avocado, and oils provide fats that support energy storage, protection, and vitamin absorption.
7. **Water for Hydration** — Water supports temperature control, transport, joints, and waste removal.
8. **Another Grain Choice** — Rice, oats, corn, breads, noodles, and other grains can be part of a balanced meal.
9. **Protein Variety** — Fish, tofu, eggs, beans, lentils, meat, nuts, dairy, and alternatives can contribute protein.
10. **Fruit and Vegetable Mix** — A variety of fruits and vegetables brings different tastes, textures, and micronutrients.
11. **Treats Can Fit** — A treat can be part of eating without becoming the foundation of the meal.
12. **Compare, Ask, and Choose** — Labels are evidence tools, and a trusted adult can help interpret unfamiliar ingredients or claims.
