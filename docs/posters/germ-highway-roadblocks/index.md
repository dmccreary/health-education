1. **Open Cough Route** — Droplets from an uncovered cough can carry germs through the air and onto nearby surfaces.
2. **Hand-to-Face Route** — Hands can move germs from a surface to the eyes, nose, or mouth.
3. **Shared Toy Route** — Shared items can pick up germs from one hand and carry them to another.
4. **Busy Doorknob Route** — Many hands touch doorknobs, so germs can collect even when the knob looks clean.
5. **Desk and Crayon Route** — Hands, desks, and shared supplies can form a chain that moves germs.
6. **Snack Route** — Germs on hands can reach food and then the mouth.
7. **Tissue Roadblock** — A tissue catches droplets; throwing it away keeps those droplets off hands and tables.
8. **Elbow Roadblock** — An elbow cover blocks many droplets and keeps them off the hands.
9. **Soap-and-Water Roadblock** — Soap and running water remove germs from all parts of the hands.
10. **Clean-Surface Roadblock** — Cleaning shared surfaces is a community health practice that reduces germ routes.
