1. **Notice a Change** — Pay attention to changes in mood, behavior, connection, sleep, or functioning.
2. **Choose Time and Place** — A private, calm moment can make conversation easier.
3. **Listen** — Give attention without rushing to fix, debate, or compare.
4. **Ask Directly About Safety** — If you are worried about suicide or self-harm, ask clearly and calmly.
5. **Validate** — Acknowledge that the person’s feelings and experience matter.
6. **Connect a Trusted Adult** — Bring in a safe adult who can help assess and act.
7. **Connect Professional Help** — Counselors, clinicians, and crisis professionals can assess risk and provide care.
8. **Respond to Urgent Crisis** — Immediate danger requires emergency or crisis help now.
9. **Keep Boundaries** — A caring friend is not a therapist or sole safety plan.
10. **Follow Up** — A later message or conversation shows that care continues.
11. **Care for Yourself** — Supporting someone can be emotionally demanding.
12. **Build a Support Team** — Several caring people and services create a stronger safety net.
