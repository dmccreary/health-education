# Families: Many Ways to Care — Image Generation Prompt

Please generate a new 1536 × 1024 px wide-landscape illustration.

## Critical rule

**The image must contain absolutely no text, labels, arrows, numbers, title, heading, callout marks, readable book pages, logos, signatures, or watermarks.** The HTML overlay supplies all annotation.

## Scene

Create one coherent sunny neighborhood-park picnic showing many different families caring for one another. Use ultra-high-quality modern picture-book illustration, softly dimensional digital gouache, crisp silhouettes, warm expressions, and culturally inclusive detail.

Represent varied races and cultures, one-parent and two-parent groupings, grandparents and relatives, blended or foster-family groupings, two moms or two dads, varied bodies and abilities, and a child who uses a wheelchair. No family should appear more central or “normal” than another.

## Eight callout targets

1. At roughly 11%, 71%: an adult and child spread a colorful picnic blanket together.
2. At roughly 23%, 54%: family members share foods from several cultural traditions at a low table.
3. At roughly 36%, 27%: a grandparent reads a blank picture book to children beneath a tree.
4. At roughly 48%, 70%: caring adults help a child with a sun hat and sunscreen.
5. At roughly 59%, 31%: an adult kneels at a child's level, listening and offering a gentle side hug.
6. At roughly 70%, 72%: a child using a wheelchair and other children roll or toss a bright ball together.
7. At roughly 82%, 53%: a caregiver pours water into a child's cup at a picnic table.
8. At roughly 91%, 28%: two family groups greet one another beneath a shade canopy and make room for new arrivals.

Arrange these as a continuous community scene, not panels. Keep targets visually distinct and 7–9 percentage points apart. Use paths, trees, blankets, tables, and shade to create rhythm without arrows or dividers.

Do not create posed portraits, a family taxonomy chart, matching outfits, stereotypes, sadness, conflict, danger, or pity-based disability framing. Focus on observable care, joy, respect, safety, and belonging.
