1. **Notice Body Signals** — A nervous stomach, tight muscles, or a strong urge to leave can be useful information.
2. **Recognize Peer Pressure** — Peer pressure can be direct or as subtle as “everyone else is doing it.”
3. **Spot a Trusted Helper** — Knowing where trusted people are is part of calm situational awareness.
4. **Keep a Public Way Out** — Notice exits and well-used public routes before you need them.
5. **Use a Direct No** — A short, clear refusal can be enough: “No thanks, I’m not doing that.”
6. **Suggest Something Safer** — An alternative can redirect a group toward a safe or kind activity.
7. **Move Toward Safe People** — Choose a public place with safe people rather than becoming isolated.
8. **Contact a Trusted Adult** — Call or message an established trusted adult and explain where you are.
9. **Tell What Happened** — Tell a trusted adult even if the situation ended or you are unsure how serious it was.
10. **Reach a Safe Destination** — A practiced route ends with safety and support, not with handling everything alone.
