    Interactive Infographic Poster Ideas¶

    This catalog draws on all 609 concepts in the eight age-band learning graphs. It complements the existing Food Groups and Plate Proportions posters rather than repeating them. Each idea is intended for a wide-landscape, high-quality illustrated poster with Explore and Quiz modes.

    Design key¶

    • Grid — two to six large rectangular regions or columns. Printed region headings may be part of the artwork; the interactive overlay supplies the explanations and quiz behavior.
    • Callout — five to fifteen point markers on a single illustrated scene or scientific image. The artwork should contain no title, labels, arrows, numbers, or other annotation text because the overlay supplies them.
    • Kindergarten posters should be image-led and read-aloud friendly. Complexity, text density, and analytical demand should rise gradually through the later bands.
    • Cultural practices should be represented as valid, varied approaches rather than as decorative side notes. People and families should reflect varied cultures, abilities, bodies, and family structures.

    Kindergarten¶

    Poster idea Overlay Wide-landscape artwork and interaction Concept connections
    My Body's Daily Helpers — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five bright picture-book stations show drinking water, eating varied foods, washing up, active play, and balanced technology time. Students open each station to hear what it helps the body do. Hydration; hunger signal; personal care practice; physical activity; daily movement; healthy technology use
    Feelings Weather Station — DONE Callout: 8 markers A playful sky changes across a panorama: sunny, cloudy, stormy, windy, and calm moments are paired with expressive children and a nearby trusted adult. Markers name feelings and identify when help is useful. Feelings; naming feelings; strong feelings; prolonged sadness; emotions vocabulary; asking for help; trusted adult
    Kindness Makes Room — DONE Grid: 4 zones A playground story unfolds as “Notice,” “Invite,” “Play Together,” and “Help Repair.” Explore mode reveals kind choices; quiz mode asks which action helps someone belong. Kindness; kind and unkind behavior; including others; family and community belonging
    The Germ's Busy Day — DONE Callout: 10 markers One mischievous cartoon germ travels through a classroom from cough to hand to toy to snack, while handwashing and covering coughs block its route. Germs; handwashing; covering coughs; personal care; community health practices
    Where Is My Safe Place? — DONE Grid: 4 zones A continuous home-school-playground-community panorama contains four clearly distinct settings. Students explore safe places, rules, helpers, and actions for each setting. Safety rules; injury prevention; community safety practice; school emergency; emergency; safe place
    Needs, Wants, and Healthy Choices — DONE Grid: 3 zones Three large illustrated baskets—“My Body Needs,” “Things I Want,” and “A Choice I Can Make”—contain familiar objects and activities. Quiz prompts ask where examples belong. Personal health needs and wants; health decision; personal health goal; positive and negative influences
    Families: Many Ways to Care — DONE Callout: 8 markers A neighborhood picnic shows varied family structures sharing food, comfort, movement, stories, and help. Each marker focuses on an act of care instead of defining one family as the norm. Family; family structure; cultural food tradition; trusted adults; kindness

    Grade 1¶

    Poster idea Overlay Wide-landscape artwork and interaction Concept connections
    Breakfast Around the Table — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five breakfast tables from varied cultural traditions show food variety, water and other drinks, safe preparation, and safe storage. Students explore what each table contributes to a healthy start. Food groups; variety of foods; breakfast; drink types; water; food preparation, storage, and safety
    Friendship Garden — DONE Callout: 10 markers A lush community garden turns friendship traits into visible actions: sharing tools, listening, welcoming differences, repairing mistakes, and asking for space. Peer relationships; healthy peer traits; uniqueness; belonging; caring relationships; kindness
    From Feeling to Words — DONE Grid: 4 zones A comic-strip panorama moves from “What Happened?” to “What I Feel,” “What I Can Say,” and “Who Can Help.” Quiz mode asks students to match helpful words to situations. Situational feelings; talking about feelings; communicating feelings and needs; recognizing need for help
    Germ Highway and Roadblocks — DONE Callout: 10 markers A classroom is styled like a tiny road map. Germ routes run through hands, surfaces, shared items, coughs, and food; hygiene actions appear as roadblocks. Germs; how germs spread; hygiene; illness prevention action; community health practice
    Pause Before You Share — DONE Grid: 4 zones Four device screens show “Learn,” “Play,” “Private Information,” and “Ask an Adult.” Students uncover why an example is safe, needs a pause, or needs help. Healthy technology use; sharing information safely; safe and unsafe behavior; asking a trusted adult
    My Safety Voice — DONE Grid: 4 zones Four strong visual panels show “Notice,” “Say No,” “Move Away,” and “Tell a Trusted Adult.” The scenarios cover permission, body safety, and unsafe behavior without depicting frightening detail. Permission; body safety; unsafe touch; saying no; seeking help; trusted adult
    Active Play Adventure Park — DONE Callout: 9 markers An inclusive fantasy park contains climbing, dancing, wheeling, stretching, playground games, and quiet recovery. Markers reveal physical and emotional benefits of each activity. Physical activity; benefits of activity; daily active play; valuing uniqueness

    Grade 2¶

    Poster idea Overlay Wide-landscape artwork and interaction Concept connections
    Drink Detective Lab — DONE Grid: 4 zones A colorful investigation bench compares water, milk or culturally familiar nourishing drinks, sugary drinks, and caffeinated drinks. Students inspect clues about energy, sugar, caffeine, and everyday choices. Sugar and caffeine in drinks; comparing drink choices; food energy; food group function
    Kitchen-to-Lunchbox Safety Chain — DONE Callout: 12 markers A continuous kitchen counter follows food through clean, separate, prepare, cook, cool, store, pack, and eat steps. Hidden trouble spots become quiz targets. Food storage, preparation, and handling safety
    The Conflict Toolbox — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five oversized toolbox drawers hold calming strategies, I-statements, active listening, solution ideas, and repair actions. Students select the tool a scenario needs. Conflict; healthy conflict resolution; strong emotions; I-statements; active listening; decision outcomes
    Personal-Space Planets — DONE Callout: 9 markers Children orbit in a whimsical playground solar system with differently sized “space bubbles.” Markers explore asking, noticing signals, playful interaction, teasing, and respectful adjustment. Personal space; boundary; playful interaction; teasing; effects of teasing; peer relationships
    Outdoor Health Shield — DONE Grid: 5 zones A giant shield is divided into sun, sound, chemicals, water, and germs. Each illustrated zone shows a risk on one side and a protective action on the other. Environmental health risks; sun and noise protection; unsafe chemicals and water; illness prevention
    Safe Tech: Stop, Close, Tell — DONE Grid: 3 zones Three bold panels turn a simple response into a visual sequence for pop-ups, unwanted messages, requests for information, and upsetting content. Technology safety; unsafe technology situations; getting help; encouraging safe choices
    Goal Trail Map — DONE Callout: 8 markers A friendly trail map moves from choosing a goal through small actions, helpers, obstacles, check-ins, feelings, and celebration. Goal setting; goal-supporting actions; reflection; health decision options and outcomes; positive modeling

    Grade 3¶

    Poster idea Overlay Wide-landscape artwork and interaction Concept connections
    The Body Fuel Team — DONE Grid: 4 zones Four energetic “team stations” compare carbohydrates, protein, fats, and hydration, using familiar foods and activities instead of ranking foods as good or bad. Carbohydrates; protein; fats; beverages for physical activity; personal choices and wellbeing
    Hunger and Fullness Dashboard — DONE Callout: 9 markers A child-centered body dashboard shows gentle internal and situational clues before, during, and after eating. Students distinguish body signals from external prompts without prescribing portion sizes. Hunger and fullness signals; personal choices; wellbeing; food and nutrition
    Read the Label, Solve the Snack — DONE Callout: 10 markers A large fictional nutrition label sits beside three snack situations—school day, active afternoon, and long outing. Markers reveal serving information, nutrients, sugar, and caffeine clues. Nutrition label; limiting sugary and caffeinated drinks; beverages for activity
    Growing Up: Everyone's Timeline Is Different — DONE Callout: 10 markers A respectful, inclusive timeline shows general physical, emotional, and social changes without assigning a single “normal” schedule. Help resources appear throughout. Puberty; stages of puberty; asking for help with body changes; trusted adults
    The Empathy Bridge — DONE Grid: 4 zones A visual bridge crosses from “Notice” through “Imagine,” “Ask and Listen,” to “Include and Act.” Scenes address exclusion, bullying, disability inclusion, and friendship. Empathy; empathy in friendships; bullying; exclusion; kindness and respect for all abilities
    Before You Send — DONE Grid: 4 zones A message travels through “Pause,” “Check Privacy,” “Consider Impact,” and “Choose.” Explore mode reveals risks in images, location clues, personal details, and tone. Sharing messages and images; personal-information risk; communication; decision-making
    Health Information Detective Desk — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five evidence folders compare a trusted adult, health professional, official public-health source, ad or influencer, and anonymous post. Students inspect author, evidence, motive, date, and fit. Validity and reliability of health information; influences on behavior; determining need for assistance

    Grade 4¶

    Poster idea Overlay Wide-landscape artwork and interaction Concept connections
    Nutrient Crew: Different Jobs, One Body — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five illustrated departments show carbohydrates, protein, fats, vitamins and minerals, and water doing distinct jobs in one bustling “body city.” Macronutrients; carbohydrates; protein; fats; vitamins and minerals; hydration
    Build a Balanced Meal from Label Clues — DONE Callout: 12 markers A market-to-table panorama pairs fictional package labels with fresh and culturally varied foods. Markers reveal useful label clues and balanced-meal reasoning. Nutrition labels; balanced meal planning; sugary and salty foods; caffeine; hydration
    Three Kinds of Health Defense — DONE Grid: 3 zones Three columns compare infectious disease prevention, non-infectious disease risk reduction, and allergy management, emphasizing that each needs a different response. Infectious and non-infectious disease; allergies; germ spread; prevention and management
    Pressure Escape Route — DONE Callout: 10 markers A branching school-and-community panorama includes early warning signs, peer pressure, unsafe locations, exits, refusal words, and trusted helpers. Warning signs; situational awareness; peer pressure; refusal skills; exiting unsafe situations
    From Bias to Belonging — DONE Grid: 4 zones Four panels move from recognizing bias and prejudice to interrupting teasing, responding to bullying, and creating belonging across cultural differences. Bias; prejudice; teasing and exclusion; bullying response; respecting cultural differences
    Calm Is More Than One Thing — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five culturally varied regulation practices—breathing, movement, art or music, time in nature, and connection—are shown without presenting one universal solution. Self-regulation; cultural self-regulation; managing emotions; mental-health benefits of culture
    Can I Trust This Health Claim? — DONE Grid: 5 zones A “claim testing studio” compares evidence, source expertise, sales motive, recency, and cultural or personal fit. Quiz prompts use fictional products and media claims. Valid and reliable information; media influence; evaluating products and services; research-based positions

    Grade 5¶

    Poster idea Overlay Wide-landscape artwork and interaction Concept connections
    Sleep: The Overnight Repair Shop — DONE Callout: 10 markers A cross-section of a whimsical overnight repair shop shows memory sorting, body growth, emotion reset, immune support, and morning attention. Sleep and rest needs; growth and learning; personal behavior and long-term health
    The Fitness Component Field Day — DONE Grid: 4 zones Four inclusive activity stations illustrate cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength, flexibility, and balance and coordination, matching the components taught in the Grade 5 chapter. Fitness components; activities by fitness component; health ownership
    Allergy Action: Notice, Protect, Get Help — DONE Grid: 4 zones Four high-contrast panels show common signs, stopping exposure, finding an adult or medication, and recognizing an emergency. Allergic reactions and signs; response; knowing when to seek help; emergency risk reduction
    Consent and Coercion Are Not the Same — DONE Grid: 4 zones Respectful scenario cards compare freely given agreement, uncertainty, boundary-setting, and pressure or coercion. The overlay explains voice, body language, power, and the right to change one's mind. Consent; coercion; boundaries; recognizing and reporting unsafe situations
    Upstander Pathways — DONE Callout: 10 markers One school scene offers several safe ways to respond to exclusion, bullying, or possible fighting: include, interrupt, distract, document, get help, and support afterward. Bystander action; equity; belonging; inclusive environments; violence prevention
    Digital Relationship Control Room — DONE Grid: 5 zones A control-room metaphor covers privacy, pressure, social-media harm, reporting, and safely exiting or seeking support in an unhealthy relationship. Social media harm; technology-harm prevention; reporting; unhealthy relationships; boundary-setting
    Choices, Consequences, and Course Corrections — DONE Callout: 9 markers A branching bike-route map shows influences, decision options, short- and long-term consequences, reflection, support, and changing course. Family and peer influence; health decisions and consequences; goal tracking; ownership of health

    Grades 6–8¶

    Poster idea Overlay Wide-landscape artwork and interaction Concept connections
    Food Choices Through Five Lenses — DONE Grid: 5 zones The same meal is examined through nutrition labels, marketing claims, cultural dietary guidance, food access, and food sovereignty. Students see why a food choice is more than a nutrient score. Food-label and marketing analysis; cultural guidelines; food sovereignty; community food access
    Consent Communication: Clear, Ongoing, Reversible — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five scenario columns show clear agreement, checking in, uncertainty, withdrawal, and coercion. Explore mode emphasizes boundaries, respect, power, and support. Consent; coercion; power dynamics; boundary communication; healthy relationships
    A Teen's Map to Health Care — DONE Callout: 12 markers A town-map journey links school staff, clinics, telehealth, pharmacies, sexual-health services, transportation, interpreters, cost support, and trusted adults. Comprehensive and adolescent healthcare; access barriers; professional support; STI and pregnancy prevention
    Stress Signal to Support Network — DONE Callout: 12 markers A split body-and-community panorama connects stress signals and emotional-distress warnings to restorative practices, peer support, trusted adults, professionals, and crisis help. Stressors; distress and suicide warning signs; coping; help-seeking; empathy; cultural healing
    The Developing Brain Under Different Influences — DONE Grid: 6 zones Six evidence-focused columns compare sleep, exercise, alcohol, nicotine or commercial tobacco, cannabis, and medication misuse. Artwork avoids scare imagery and moral labeling. Brain-healthy habits; sleep; physical activity; substance effects; medication misuse; injury risk
    Emergency Response Field Guide — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five illustrated cards cover scene safety, recognizing an emergency, calling for help, choking response, and emergency medication. Quiz mode asks for the safest next action. Scene safety; priority needs; Heimlich maneuver; emergency medication; school safety actions
    Recovery Is an Ecosystem — DONE Callout: 12 markers A landscape ecosystem shows treatment, peers, family, culture, community, healthy coping, policy, relapse response, and renewed goals as connected supports rather than a single path. Substance use disorder; treatment; relapse; cultural recovery support; resilience; health policy
    Sleep Pattern Influences Lab — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five luminous lab stations compare body-clock timing, light and screens, schedule and stress, caffeine and other substances, and the sleep environment. Circadian rhythm; sleep hygiene; screen use; stress; caffeine; sleep environment
    Medication Safety Cabinet — DONE Callout: 10 markers A home-to-pharmacy scene covers label reading, the correct person and timing, interactions, professional questions, secure storage, no sharing, disposal, and emergency help. Medication safety; prescription misuse; safe storage; pharmacist; poison response
    Digital Boundary Checkpoints — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five device-safety panels move from recognizing pressure through stating a boundary, preserving evidence, blocking or reporting, and getting support. Digital pressure; boundaries; evidence preservation; reporting; trusted adults
    Stigma to Support — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five social scenes replace mental-health stigma with checked assumptions, respectful language, connection to care, and deliberate belonging. Mental-health stigma; respectful language; help-seeking; inclusion; belonging
    Workout Design Studio — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five sports-science stations build a balanced activity plan from a goal and baseline through endurance, strength, mobility, recovery, and revision. Fitness planning; endurance; strength; mobility; recovery; self-monitoring
    Impaired Driving Risk Web — DONE Callout: 10 markers A dusk-to-safe-arrival journey connects impaired attention, reaction, and judgment with passengers, restraints, alternate rides, key intervention, consequences, and emergency help. Impaired driving; substance effects; passenger safety; seatbelts; refusal and help-seeking

    Grades 9–12¶

    Poster idea Overlay Wide-landscape artwork and interaction Concept connections
    Personal Nutrition Design Studio — DONE Grid: 6 zones Six design panels move from personal needs and preferences through label evidence, beverage evidence, meal timing, cultural traditions, and plan revision. Nutrition assessment and planning; nutrient and beverage evaluation; culture; adapting health behavior
    Health Opportunity: What a Neighborhood Makes Easier or Harder — DONE Callout: 15 markers Two adjoining neighborhoods reveal differences in food access, recreation, transit, clinics, pollution, safety, cost, disability access, and community initiatives without blaming residents. Social conditions and health disparities; systemic inequity; physical-activity access; food systems; community wellbeing
    Youth-Friendly Care: Rights, Respect, and Access — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five clinic “doors” cover rights and confidentiality, culturally safe care, STI and sexual-health services, mental-health care, and practical access supports. Adolescent access rights; youth-friendly providers; culturally safe care; stigma; healthcare resources
    Consent in Context — DONE Grid: 6 zones Six scenario columns examine clear communication, nonverbal ambiguity, withdrawal, power differences, digital contexts, and legal context. The interaction asks what information or action is still needed. Consent; withdrawal; power dynamics; law; boundaries; negotiation and communication evaluation
    Support Without Carrying It Alone — DONE Callout: 12 markers A peer-support panorama shows noticing changes, listening, asking directly, connecting to help, responding to crisis, maintaining boundaries, following up, and caring for oneself. Depression and anxiety; suicide or self-harm help; peer crisis support; treatment; empathy; boundaries
    CPR, AED, and the Chain of Survival — DONE Callout: 12 markers A clear overhead emergency scene marks scene safety, responsiveness, emergency activation, compressions, AED retrieval, pad placement, analysis, shock clearance, and handoff. CPR techniques; AED use; online or school emergency response; evaluating safety practices
    From Evidence to Health Action — DONE Grid: 6 zones A campaign studio moves from verifying information through mapping influences, comparing options, setting goals, tailoring a message, and evaluating results. Students inspect how a health idea becomes an ethical, evidence-backed intervention. Information validity; multilevel influences; decision-making; goal strategies; message design; promotion effectiveness
    Public Health Influence Map — DONE Callout: 12 markers A community systems landscape surrounds students with family and culture, peers, school, media, care, neighborhood design, transportation, food access, policy, economics, environment, and advocacy. Social-ecological influences; policy; economics; environment; community advocacy
    Mental Health Media Decoder — DONE Grid: 5 zones Five media-literacy panels examine representation, language, evidence, audience impact, and redesigning a more humane and accurate message. Media literacy; mental-health representation; evidence; stigma; message design
    Health Goal Feedback Loop — DONE Callout: 9 markers A circular studio follows assessment, values, goals, small actions, support, tracking, reflection, barrier adaptation, and revision. Goal setting; self-monitoring; social support; barriers; behavior-change feedback

    Suggested first production set¶

    For an initial collection with one poster per band, these offer strong visual variety and broad curricular value:

    1. Kindergarten — The Germ's Busy Day
    2. Grade 1 — Friendship Garden
    3. Grade 2 — The Conflict Toolbox
    4. Grade 3 — Health Information Detective Desk
    5. Grade 4 — Three Kinds of Health Defense
    6. Grade 5 — Sleep: The Overnight Repair Shop
    7. Grades 6–8 — Food Choices Through Five Lenses
    8. Grades 9–12 — Health Opportunity: What a Neighborhood Makes Easier or Harder

    This starter set alternates grid and callout engines, spans physical, mental, social, environmental, and information health, and avoids duplicating the two nutrition posters already present.
